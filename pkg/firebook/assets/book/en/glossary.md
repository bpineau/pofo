# The FIRE and withdrawal glossary
<!-- source: lexique @ 773915a72b6f -->

Every term in this book, and the forum jargon around it, defined in a few sentences, with a pointer to the chapter that covers it. A glossary is something you look things up in, but this one is also meant to be **read**. Straight through, it works as a summary of the book in alphabetical order. The last section pulls the thirteen numbers that turn up everywhere into one table, so you can check your ballpark at a glance.

## A-B

**ABW (amortization based withdrawal)**: an actuarial withdrawal rule. Each year you pay yourself the amount that would exhaust total wealth (the portfolio plus the discounted value of future flows, minus the bequest you are aiming at) over the remaining horizon, at the current expected return. A mortgage run backwards, recomputed every year. [[amortization-based-withdrawal]]

**All Weather**: Ray Dalio's all-weather portfolio. It balances the risk contributions of the four growth x inflation regimes. [[all-weather-portfolios]]

**Anarkulova-Cederburg**: the work that recomputes the withdrawal rate over a century of developed markets (38 countries). A rigid 4% fails about 17% of the time there. The modern prudent bound. [[anarkulova-cederburg]]

**Annuity**: capital converted into an income paid for as long as you live. Longevity insurance, whose own return comes from mortality credits. [[annuities-and-safety-first]]

**Asset allocation**: how the portfolio is split between classes (stocks, bonds, gold and the rest). In the withdrawal phase, a wide plateau (50 to 80% stocks) that drops away on both sides. [[stock-bond-allocation]]

**Backwardation / contango**: the two possible slopes of a futures curve. Rolling your positions pays in backwardation (distant contracts trade below spot) and costs in contango, which is what dooms naive commodity trackers. [[global-macro]]

**Barista FIRE**: capital covers part of your spending, work you actually want covers the rest. The side benefits of a little income make it worth more than the paycheck alone. [[what-is-fire]], [[going-back-to-work]]

**Bengen rule**: the fixed withdrawal indexed to inflation. The founding rule (1994), and the yardstick every comparison is measured against. [[fixed-inflation-adjusted-withdrawal]], [[the-trinity-study]]

**Block bootstrap**: resampling history by drawing multi-year blocks and gluing them together. Synthetic histories made of real pieces. [[historical-vs-parametric]]

**Bond tent**: raising the bond share as the exit approaches, then bringing it back down through the first decade. A peak of caution over the fragile window. [[glidepaths]]

**Breakeven**: the gap between the nominal yield and the real yield. The inflation the market expects. The only forecast with money behind it. [[inflation-linked-bonds]], [[tracking-inflation]]

**Bridge (the uncovered phase)**: the years between your exit and your pensions, when the portfolio funds everything. The real horizon at risk in a FIRE plan. Its length governs how complicated the plan has to be. [[horizon-and-life-expectancy]]

**Broad sample**: the model that replays a century of 16 developed countries (the JST panel, 1870-2020) in a domestic 60/40. The empirical bound on long-horizon risk. [[anarkulova-cederburg]], [[under-the-hood]]

**Buckets**: cutting wealth into horizons (cash, bonds, stocks). An allocation in disguise, plus a set of flows you still have to write down. Good language, no mechanical value of its own. [[the-bucket-strategy]]

## C-D

**CAPE (the Shiller ratio)**: price divided by average real earnings over 10 years. The best known predictor of real returns 10 to 15 years out, and therefore of what a starting vintage is in for. [[valuations-and-cape]]

**CAPE rule**: withdrawal rate = a + b × (1/CAPE), applied to the portfolio as it stands. The percentage rule, made aware of valuations. Self-smoothing, through its double countercyclicality. [[cape-based-rules]]

**Carry**: the premium you collect by holding what pays carry, funded by what does not (currencies, yield curves, commodities). Documented across every asset class, cyclical, and fragile in a liquidity crisis. [[global-macro]]

**Cascade (of returns)**: arithmetic > geometric > real > net > sustainable. Every step costs 0.5 to 1.5 points. The demystification of the 4% rule. [[arithmetic-vs-geometric-returns]]

**Cash buffer**: 18 to 36 months of spending held in cash, drawn on below a written drawdown threshold and refilled in calm weather. Quantitatively neutral, behaviorally precious. [[cash-buffer]], [[refilling-the-buffer]]

**Certainty equivalent**: the guaranteed income you would accept in exchange for a risky plan. The universal converter between distributions, and what deflates a pretty average with an ugly tail. [[deciding-under-uncertainty]]

**Coast FIRE**: the capital will get there on its own, by compounding. You stop saving and work to cover current spending. [[what-is-fire]], [[going-back-to-work]]

**Cohorts, vintages**: start dates replayed one by one through history. Bengen's method. The worst American vintage is 1966, not 1929. [[the-trinity-study]]

**CPI-U**: the US consumer price index, the standard deflator for real returns and the reference for indexed spending. The euro-area equivalent is the HICP. [[tracking-inflation]]

**Current withdrawal rate**: annual spending divided by the portfolio as it stands today. The main warning light on the dashboard, graded green, amber, red. [[when-to-worry]]

**Decumulation**: the phase where you live off capital. What this book is about, and a different problem from accumulation, because the order of returns starts to matter. [[the-three-phases]]

**Decumulation frontier**: every withdrawal rule plotted as a point in the plane (failure probability against the variability of your standard of living). None wins on both axes at once, so choosing a rule means choosing where you sit along the frontier. [[withdrawal-strategies-overview]]

**df (degrees of freedom)**: the parameter that sets tail thickness in the central model's Student-t. The lower df is, the more often extreme years show up (at df 5, about 10 times more catastrophic years than under a normal distribution). [[fat-tails]], [[under-the-hood]]

**Die With Zero**: Perkins's thesis. Dying rich is a failure. In its reasonable version: dated time buckets and generosity while you are alive, applied to the surplus of a plan that is already safe. [[spending-in-retirement]]

**Drawdown**: the decline from the last peak, measured in real terms for a retiree. The standard trigger for drawing on the buffer (10 to 20%). [[refilling-the-buffer]]

**Duration**: a bond's sensitivity to rates (roughly the percentage it loses per point of rate rise), and the horizon over which its yield is locked in. [[bonds-in-retirement]]

## E-G

**ERN (Early Retirement Now)**: Karsten Jeske's blog and its Safe Withdrawal Rate series, more than 60 parts. The modern quantitative reference on the subject. [[the-ern-series]]

**Expected utility**: the criterion that weighs each level of wealth by what it is really worth to you, rather than in raw dollars. Its concavity is where risk aversion comes from, and why a plan gets judged on its 5th percentile plus its median. [[deciding-under-uncertainty]]

**Failure probability**: the share of simulated futures where the portfolio runs dry before the horizon, under a mechanical rule. A property of the plan and the model together. Read it as a range, and as a ranking. [[failure-probability]]

**Fan chart**: the distribution of simulated wealth by date, in percentile bands. A stack of cross-sections, not a bundle of paths. [[reading-a-fan-chart]]

**Financial repression**: holding the return paid on savings below inflation for years on end. Historically the way governments prefer to get out of debt. The regime that erodes without ever setting off an alarm. [[hyperinflation-and-extremes]]

**Fixed percentage**: withdrawing w% of the portfolio as it stands. Running out of capital becomes impossible; lifestyle ruin does not. The raw material of the modern rules, to be smoothed (Yale). [[fixed-percentage]]

**Floor**: the budget you can hold for five years, morale included. The plan's real capacity for flexibility. Tested, in a trial quarter, never simply declared. [[flexibility-in-practice]], [[how-much-you-need]]

**Fragile window**: the first 5 to 10 years of withdrawals, where sequence risk is concentrated. The stretch the buffer, the bond tent and early income exist to protect. [[sequence-of-returns]]

**Geometric mean**: the growth rate you actually compound at. Always below the arithmetic mean, by about σ²/2 (the volatility drag). Your wealth lives in geometric terms. [[arithmetic-vs-geometric-returns]]

**Glide path**: letting the allocation move over time. The withdrawal-phase version (rising equity: lifting the stock share as you come out of the fragile window) is backed by Pfau-Kitces and by ERN. [[glidepaths]]

**Global macro**: funds that bet on the big economic forces (rates, currencies, commodities) rather than on individual securities. Low correlation and good crisis vintages, but enormous dispersion between managers, and the best funds are closed to individuals. [[global-macro]]

**Golden Butterfly**: Browne plus a small-cap value wing. The best SWR-to-drawdown pair in the all-weather family over the sample available. [[all-weather-portfolios]]

**Guardrails**: a stable withdrawal, adjusted by plus or minus 10% whenever an indicator leaves its corridor. The modern indicator is the re-simulated success probability (Kitces-Tharp, Morningstar). [[guyton-klinger]], [[morningstar-guardrails]]

## H-M

**Hysteresis**: trigger at one threshold, disarm at a lower one. What keeps every warning light in the plan from crying wolf. [[when-to-worry]]

**i.i.d. (independent and identically distributed)**: draws with no memory, each period independent and pulled from the same distribution. Naive Monte Carlo. It under-represents long mediocre stretches (no clustering, no return of valuations toward their mean), which is why the engines with memory exist. [[monte-carlo-strengths-and-limits]], [[making-monte-carlo-relevant]]

**Kelly criterion**: the fraction of capital that maximizes the geometric growth of a repeated bet. Brilliant at the casino, unusable as it stands for a retirement (finite horizon, consumption, unknown parameters). What survives is the half-Kelly reflex. [[deciding-under-uncertainty]]

**Ladder (bonds, linkers)**: one rung held to maturity per year of liability. The matching that cancels interest-rate risk, and the contractual version of the bridge to your pensions. [[bond-ladders]], [[inflation-linked-bonds]]

**Lean / Fat FIRE**: the frugal variant ($15,000 to $25,000 a year) and the comfortable one ($60,000 and well beyond). The difficulty between the two is not linear. [[what-is-fire]]

**Linkers (inflation-linked bonds)**: TIPS and their foreign cousins. The only contract written in real terms: inflation, plus a guaranteed real yield. The retiree's conceptual risk-free asset. [[inflation-linked-bonds]]

**Long volatility, tail hedging**: holding convexity (puts) that pays off in a fast crash, against a premium paid all the time. Negative expectancy by construction, the theoretical complement to trend, and almost impossible to buy cleanly in a fund. [[long-volatility]]

**Managed futures, trend**: systematic trend-following strategies, long and short, across asset classes. The only defensive holding with a positive expectancy, and the winner of long regimes (2008, 2022). [[managed-futures]]

**Market regimes**: the persistent seasons that cross growth and inflation. A 60/40 covers two of them. Stagflation is the retiree's nightmare. [[market-regimes]]

**Monte Carlo**: running the plan through thousands of futures drawn from a model. A conditional theorem ("if the world draws from this urn..."), never a prediction. [[monte-carlo-strengths-and-limits]]

**Mortality credits**: the share of an annuity funded by the deaths of the other policyholders. They grow with age, which is the reason to annuitize late. [[annuities-and-safety-first]]

**mu (μ)**: the annualized real arithmetic mean of the return engine (the location parameter of the Student-t). What you actually compound is about μ − σ²/2. Enter it modest and **real**, never the average off a brochure. [[under-the-hood]], [[arithmetic-vs-geometric-returns]]

## O-R

**OMY (one more year)**: the syndrome of the extra year. Putting the exit off for want of a number that finally feels like enough. The veterans' number one regret. Treated with criteria written in advance, while calm. [[one-more-year]]

**Permanent Portfolio**: Browne's four quarters (stocks, long bonds, gold, cash). The all-weather archetype. One winner per regime, rebalanced. [[all-weather-portfolios]]

**Portable alpha**: the institutional technique that carries a source of return on top of a beta obtained through futures. The ancestor of return stacking. [[return-stacking]]

**Rebalancing premium (diversification return)**: the extra geometric return a rebalanced basket of uncorrelated assets earns over the average of its components, roughly half the variance it saves (0.2 to 0.5 points a year is typical). [[why-diversification-works]]

**Retirement smile**: the shape of real spending. High at the start, declining for twenty years, rising at the end with health care. A budget built on a constant need over-provisions by 10 to 20%. [[spending-in-retirement]]

**Return stacking**: putting moderate leverage inside funds built for it (90/60, stocks plus trend) so you hold the diversifiers on top of the core instead of in its place. Every stacked layer earns its own return minus cash. [[return-stacking]]

**Risk premium**: the wage of risk carried, and the reason any return exists at all. Assets that hurt at the worst moment (stocks) pay. Assets that transfer no risk to anyone (gold, cash) earn nothing in real terms, by construction. [[risk-premia]]

## S-Z

**SAFEMAX**: the highest withdrawal rate that would have survived every historical vintage (Bengen). About 4% over 30 years in the United States, about 3.25 to 3.5% at FIRE horizons. [[the-trinity-study]]

**Safety-first**: the school of liability matching. Guarantee the floor with lifetime income (pension, annuity, ladder), and risk only the comfort layer. [[annuities-and-safety-first]], [[withdrawal-strategies-overview]]

**Sequence risk**: once you are withdrawing, the order of returns matters as much as their average. Early losses become permanent. The central concept of this book. [[sequence-of-returns]]

**Sequence stress**: the sticky-bears model (Markov, mean-preserving). The gap between it and the central case measures what a bad sequence costs in your plan. [[making-monte-carlo-relevant]], [[under-the-hood]]

**sigma (σ)**: the annualized volatility of the engine, the standard deviation of its returns. Its σ²/2 drag is what separates the arithmetic mean from the geometric one (the volatility drag). Lower than the one-year vol quoted in brochures. [[arithmetic-vs-geometric-returns]], [[fat-tails]]

**Squeeze (the)**: inflation lifting indexed withdrawals while the nominal portfolio stalls. The mechanism behind the 1966 vintage. [[inflation-and-withdrawal-rates]]

**Stock-bond correlation**: the unstable parameter at the center of the allocation. Negative in a disinflationary regime (the cushion), positive in an inflationary one (2022). [[bonds-in-retirement]]

**Student-t (df)**: the fat-tailed distribution the central model draws from. df sets how often extreme years occur (at df 5, about 10 times more catastrophic years than under a normal distribution). [[fat-tails]]

**SWR (safe withdrawal rate)**: the "safe" withdrawal rate. Never a constant. A function of the frame around it: horizon, sample, valuations, margins. [[the-4-percent-rule]]

**Time buckets**: dating what you want to do by physiological window (before 60, before 75, and so on). Whatever carries a date gets funded now. The central tool of Die With Zero. [[spending-in-retirement]]

**TPAW**: Total Portfolio Allocation and Withdrawal. The Bogleheads' full ABW. Allocation and withdrawal both computed on total wealth, discounted pensions included. [[amortization-based-withdrawal]]

**Tracking difference**: a fund's performance minus its index's. The real measure of what an ETF costs; the expense ratio is only one piece of it. [[building-it-with-us-etfs]]

**Trinity study**: the 1998 grid, rate by allocation by horizon, giving the historical success rate. Where the language of failure probability comes from. [[the-trinity-study]]

**Variance risk premium**: the persistent gap between implied volatility (the price of insurance) and realized volatility, 2 to 4 points on average. What the buyer of protection overpays, and what the seller collects. [[long-volatility]]

**Volatility drag**: the σ²/2 drag between the arithmetic and the geometric mean. Volatility is a cost of compounding, even without a crash. [[arithmetic-vs-geometric-returns]]

**VPW (variable percentage withdrawal)**: the Bogleheads' percentage that rises with age, an annuity in reverse read off a fixed table. Capital ruin never happens, consumption is deliberate, and the loss tolerance test is mandatory. [[vpw]]

## The numbers to keep in your head

Thirteen numbers run through the whole book. Here they are in one place, with the term that carries each one and the chapter that establishes it. Not one of them is new here, and that is exactly the service this table renders. A number in your own plan that looks like none of these deserves an explanation before you keep it.

| Number | What it measures | Term | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~4% over 30 years | The rigid rate that survives the worst American vintage | SAFEMAX | Trinity |
| 3.25 to 3.5% | The same rate at a FIRE horizon of 45 to 55 years | SAFEMAX | ERN |
| ~17% | The failure rate of a rigid 4% on the world sample | Broad sample | Anarkulova-Cederburg |
| 0.5 to 1.5 points | What each step of the cascade costs | Cascade | The two means |
| 50 to 80% stocks | The width of the allocation plateau | Asset allocation | Allocation |
| 4 to 6 points | The historical equity premium over cash | Risk premium | Risk premia |
| 1 to 2 points | The term premium, the least stable in the catalog | Risk premium | Risk premia |
| 0.2 to 0.5 points a year | What a rebalanced basket of uncorrelated assets earns on its own | Rebalancing premium | Diversification |
| 2 to 4 points | The average gap between implied and realized volatility | Variance risk premium | Long volatility |
| 2 to 4% real, gross | The modern expectation for a trend program | Managed futures | Trend |
| 18 to 36 months | The useful size of the cash buffer | Cash buffer | Buffer |
| ±20%, cuts of 10% | The standard corridor and the standard adjustment | Guardrails | Guyton-Klinger |
| 10 to 20% | What a constant-need budget over-provisions by | Retirement smile | Spending |
