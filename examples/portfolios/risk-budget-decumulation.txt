# Risk-budget decumulation -- every weight carries the reason it exists.
#
# STATUS: implementable today from a French retail setup (a taxable account
#   plus the equity-only tax shelters). Same family as
#   dragon-decumulation-household, one generation further: the equity block
#   moves into the tax shelters as far as the RISK budget allows, the bond
#   overlay lost on the way is rebuilt with two issuers instead of one, and
#   every line is labelled by the kind of claim that justifies it.
#   The comments keep the reasoning: mechanisms, objections examined,
#   measurements, what was given up and when to reopen a question. A
#   measurement carries its date only when its number depends on the day
#   (a simulation, a price level); git keeps the history of the rest.
#
# Idea: start from the liability (a stream of real euros over ~40 years),
#   immunize the years where a bad sequence is unrecoverable, and spend the
#   surplus on mechanisms that pay a documented premium, not on assets that
#   merely backtested well.
#
# LABELS. Each weight below is tagged by what produced it:
#   DERIVED    falls out of a calculation (the linkers dose).
#   MECHANISM  a documented premium; the dose is a judgement call.
#   HEDGE      no premium at all; the dose is what the covered regime
#              demands (gold, long duration).
#   CONSTRAINT set by a wrapper's rules or by an assumed behavioral limit.
#
# ## Lineage
#
#   Browne's Permanent Portfolio (1981) -> Cole's Dragon (Artemis, 2020)
#   -> a three-line distillation (NTSG, trend, gold) ->
#   hydra-five-engines (one line per return engine) ->
#   dragon-decumulation-household (the same design across French wrappers)
#   -> here. The step this file adds is the liability: the earlier files
#   size sleeves against a portfolio, this one sizes them against a
#   spending stream, and the two answers differ. Since then the book has
#   been re-derived twice from scratch by blind designers (s. 0, s. 12)
#   and reconverged each time. Its weights have moved once since: gold
#   15 -> 12, linkers 13 -> 16, on the price of the insurance (s. 7).
#
# ## 0. The wrappers: the shelter tranche is bounded by risk, never by tax
#
#   The setup assumed: a taxable account (CTO), a PEA and a PEA-PME
#   (equity only), and an employee savings plan (PEE) whose world-equity
#   FCPE is the one diversified fund it offers. World equity lives in the
#   PEE up to the ceiling the book's EQUITY budget sets, and not one euro
#   more. Why the PEE despite its fund charges:
#   1. Future gains pay the social levies only (18.6%), and only on exit,
#      instead of the 31.4% flat tax (PFU) paid on every sale in a taxable
#      account (rebalancing, spending): each euro of future gain keeps
#      81.4 cents, not 68.6, and switches inside the plan realize nothing.
#   2. The latent tax on the gain already accrued stays invested and
#      compounds for the holder: an interest-free loan from the state,
#      repaid pro rata at each withdrawal. That, more than the rate gap,
#      is the engine. Worked example (illustrative inputs): 100 gross in
#      the plan carrying 18 of latent tax, a 0.62%/yr plan fund against a
#      0.20% ETF, and the case most favourable to the taxable account (an
#      accumulating ETF sold once, at the end, no rebalancing): the plan
#      ends 20 years ahead by +10 / +17 / +22% of net wealth for 4 / 6 /
#      8%/yr gross growth, +6 to +15% after 10 years. Every gain realized
#      on the way in the taxable account widens the gap.
#   3. The bet is reversible AT TAX PARITY: the levies are flat, with no
#      progressivity, so leaving later costs the same rate as leaving
#      today. The converse is false: a euro taken out can never come back
#      (no new contributions once the employment ends). When in doubt,
#      keep it in, up to the equity budget.
#   In that same example the plan fund charge that would cancel the
#   advantage is 1.1 to 2.1%/yr depending on growth and horizon, against
#   0.62% actually charged all-in: a margin of two at least. Three blind
#   designers from opposed schools, without access to this file, reached
#   the same doctrine: the tranche kept is sized by the equity budget,
#   never by tax.
#   SPENDING ORDER in decumulation: taxable account first, then the PEE,
#   the PEA kept in reserve, a life-insurance contract up to its yearly
#   allowance once it is eight years old (s. 14). The PEE is the FIRST
#   DRAW whenever equity is overweight: selling its world fund pays the
#   quarter, rebalances and uses the cheaper exit in one move. To de-risk
#   without tax, switch inside the plan to its money-market fund; to
#   re-risk, the taxable account buys a world fund.
#   Annual watch on the wrapper: all-in charge (feeder plus master), the
#   account fees charged after departure, the quality of the fund range.
#   The position can be exited at any time at the flat rate: a revisable
#   decision, not a lock-in.
#
# ## 1. The numeraire: the risk-free asset is not cash
#
#   Merton, then Campbell & Viceira (Strategic Asset Allocation, 2002):
#   the risk-free asset is the one whose cash flow replicates the
#   LIABILITY. The liability here is real euros for decades. Cash is not
#   risk-free against it (it loses to inflation); a ladder of euro
#   inflation-linked bonds is.
#
#   Consequence 1, how to read the linkers line. Its low expected return
#   IS its function. It is the zero point from which every other line is a
#   deliberate departure that a premium has to pay for. Its job is not to
#   earn, but to fund the floor years it covers without selling equities,
#   tracking inflation. Judge it like insurance, on service rendered,
#   never by comparing its return to equities.
#   WHAT IT DOES NOT GUARANTEE. The line held is a FUND with no maturity
#   (rolling 1-10y linkers, no capital protection per its KID). It hedges
#   part of the inflation risk and serves as a funding reserve whose sale
#   value moves with real yields (duration ~4.5, see consequence 3); it
#   does not deliver a set real sum on a set date. Only a ladder of
#   linkers held to maturity matches redemptions to needs, and even that
#   keeps issuer risk, tax, and the gap between the index and the
#   household's own spending.
#
#   Consequence 2, why 1-10y and not all maturities. Duration ~4 against
#   ~7-8 for an all-maturity fund: 2022 showed long linkers losing 25-35%
#   DURING an inflation shock (real-rate duration), exactly when they were
#   being counted on. Short maturities buy the indexation without the
#   real-rate bet. An all-maturity linker fund is a real-rate bet, not an
#   inflation hedge.
#
#   Consequence 3, measured rather than hoped. Duration 4.5 is still
#   duration: ~1.7 times less than the all-maturity fund (7-8), not zero.
#   In 2022 the line lost more than cash in real terms (-9.0% against
#   -6.6%) because real yields rose. Over 2021-2023 it beat cash by ~1.5
#   pt/yr real, and over twenty years its real drawdown is 10 points
#   shallower (-14.5% against -25.0%). What paid the inflation actually
#   lived through was trend and gold. Judge the line over a decade, never
#   on the year of the shock.
#
#   THE TAX LEAK ON INDEXATION. France taxes the NOMINAL gain, hence the
#   indexation itself. In a taxable account at 31.4% with 2.2% inflation,
#   a linker at ~1.8% real gross nets ~0.5% real (1.8 x 0.686 - 0.314 x
#   2.2); at 5% inflation the real net turns NEGATIVE (~-0.3%). A taxable
#   linker pocket is a structurally leaky inflation hedge, and that is a
#   tax fact, not a fund choice. Cushions, in order: any indexed annuity
#   (outside the tax base), gold and trend (which paid the lived
#   inflation), the option for the progressive scale in decumulation (at
#   an 11% marginal bracket, ~28.9% all-in instead of 31.4%), and the
#   line's short duration. Read this before any urge to grow the line:
#   every added point of linkers suffers the leak.
#
# ## 2. Sizing the linkers by horizon (DERIVED)
#
#   Sequence risk only destroys on the near years, where no recovery is
#   possible; distant years have the horizon working for them. So immunize
#   the beginning and take the risk on the rest. At a NET draw on the
#   book (the floor minus any bridge income) near ~2%/yr of capital,
#   discounted at the real yield linkers actually pay (~1%), paid at
#   year end:
#     years 1-3    covered by the cash buffer, held OUTSIDE the weights
#                  (s. 14)
#     years 4-10   ~12-13% of capital          <- the floor of the dose
#     years 4-12   ~16%                        <- the dose retained
#     years 4-15   ~21%
#     years 4-40   ~57%: Bodie's answer, ruin impossible, growth
#                  abandoned. Refused.
#   A household that already owns indexed annuities (state pension,
#   indexed rent, a residual salary) subtracts their present value from
#   the floor first: owning them is what lets a floor worth ~45% of
#   capital be covered by 13 to 16% of linkers. Note that such an annuity
#   starting years out does NOT shrink this dose, because it starts after
#   the immunized window; the derivation is invariant to it, which is a
#   good reason not to reopen this line at every pension scare.
#   THE HORIZON MOVES WITH THE DRAW, THE WEIGHT DOES NOT. The table scales
#   with the net draw rate: at 2.7%/yr of capital (a smaller book, or a
#   larger floor), years 4-10 need ~17.5% and the same 16 points fund
#   years 4 to ~9 only. Recompute the covered years whenever capital or
#   spending moves; the weight itself came out of the head-to-head below,
#   not out of a target horizon.
#
#   An independent recount by a blind liability-driven designer landed on
#   the same net liability and the same duration (~26 years). Its
#   corollary is worth keeping: a French state pension is already a
#   long-duration French SOVEREIGN claim, so a France-heavy linker pocket
#   stacked on top concentrates the issuer exactly where the plan's
#   political stress sits. The fund held is ~40% France (the all-maturity
#   index would be ~41% France, 27% Italy): acceptable, but an ANNUAL
#   REVIEW item. Should the pocket ever grow, cap France at ~50% of it
#   and top up with German or Spanish linkers.
#
#   WHAT SETS THE WEIGHT: THE HEAD-TO-HEAD, NEVER AN ISOLATED SWEEP. An
#   isolated Monte-Carlo sweep of the dose over {5, 10, 15, 20} favours
#   the top of the range; the full head-to-head against a book at 20
#   points of linkers erases that edge as long as a well-paid gold line and
#   14 points of small value already cover the tail (the extra linkers then
#   cost more geometric return than they buy floor years). When gold was
#   cut from 15 to 12 (s. 7), three points needed a home: the linkers,
#   world equity, euro 25+ and mixes of them were compared on the
#   2005-2025 window and on a deep stand-in one (1987-2025), replayed and
#   then resampled under prudent priors. The gaps are small (a few tenths
#   of a floor year, 1 to 3% of the 5th-percentile terminal wealth) but
#   ordered. EQUITY is the worst home in the tail and the best in the
#   median: worst real drawdown -20.3% against -18.6% for the linkers on
#   the short window, and it raises the risk share of a sleeve that already
#   carries two thirds of the variance. EURO 25+ scores well on priors but
#   deepens a 2022 (-10.5% real against -9.2%) in the very move that cuts a
#   leg absorbing rate shocks (s. 6). THE LINKERS keep the defensive share
#   of the book, its lowest volatility of the set, and the inflation leg
#   like for like: hence 16. The tax leak of s. 1 applies to the added
#   points and is accepted with open eyes; going higher takes a new
#   head-to-head.
#
# ## 3. The premia that actually exist
#
#   Risk is only paid for against a mechanism, never against a track record.
#   EQUITY     covariance with consumption. The widest premium, the
#              soundest mechanism. Anchor its expected return on CAPE
#              (~2.5% real at a CAPE near 40), not on the historical mean.
#   TREND      the most robust documented anomaly: a century, every asset
#              class (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012; Hurst, Ooi &
#              Pedersen 2017). Mechanism: under-reaction plus risk
#              transfer. Correlation ~0 to equities, negative in crises.
#              CAVEAT: that century is a paper reconstruction. The paper
#              also shows it net of estimated transaction costs and of a
#              hypothetical 2/20 fee, but no reconstruction captures
#              every cost; investable UCITS trend (0.8-1.3% plus
#              replication error) has a markedly thinner realized Sharpe
#              and an enormous manager dispersion. The premium exists; its NET SIZE is the
#              least certain input in the whole design. Measured on this
#              file's own backcast window (2005-2025, real EUR): the
#              replication leg earned ~3.8%/yr real and the direct-manager
#              leg ~1.2%, an excess over cash of ~3.6%/yr for the pair.
#              The dispersion is not hypothetical; it is the thing the
#              two-leg structure diversifies.
#   TERM       thin today. The line stands on its hedging demand (negative
#              beta to growth shocks), not on its yield.
#   SIZE/VALUE Fama-French, contested since 2000: weaker mechanism, hence
#              a minority share INSIDE the equity sleeve.
#   GOLD       zero real expected return (Erb & Harvey 2013). Not a
#              premium, an insurance, and LOGICALLY THE SAME STATUS as
#              long duration: held for its covariance with bad states of
#              the world. Size it by the regime it covers (section 7),
#              not by a "minimum polite allocation".
#
# ## 4. The risk budget, and the behavioral cap on trend
#
#   Capital shares are not risk shares, and this file is named after the
#   difference. Capital is 42/20/12/16/10 (equity / trend / gold / linkers
#   / duration). The report's risk-budget block (Euler decomposition of
#   variance on the simulation's monthly contributions, 2005-11 to
#   2026-09, current weights) reads:
#     equity 68% of variance for 42 of capital and 57% of realized return
#     gold 11% for 12 and 18%
#     trend 11% for 20 and 15%
#     govt bonds 8% for 10 and 5%
#     linkers 4% for 16 and 4%
#   Two lessons. First, a book balanced in CAPITAL across regimes is still
#   an equity book in RISK: two thirds of the variance sits in one sleeve,
#   which is exactly what the defensive lines exist to blunt, and the
#   number to watch when the equity band drifts. Second, an Euler share
#   prices MARGINAL
#   contribution, so a sleeve that moves independently of the book
#   consumes far less variance than its standalone volatility suggests.
#   Twenty points of trend cost 11% of the risk budget here; the cap below
#   is therefore a behavioral limit, not a risk-budget constraint, and
#   should be argued as one. Gold, meanwhile, is not free: it takes as
#   much variance as it takes capital, and in this window it earned it.
#   And a share measured over twenty years hides the present one: over
#   the twelve months to 2026-09, gold's realized volatility stood near
#   27%/yr against ~16% in the long run, and 15 points of it carried
#   about 40% of the book's variance (32% at 12 points; twelve monthly
#   observations, an order of magnitude), one of the reasons for the dose
#   of s. 7. Re-run the block after any weight change (the "Risk budget"
#   row of the HTML report) rather than trusting an ex-ante estimate.
#
#   Within that budget, unconstrained optimization would push trend
#   toward 30-45 and gold lower. Refused, for two independent reasons:
#   (a) uncertainty on trend's NET premium (section 3): moving 14 points
#       of risk budget onto the least certain input is optimization under
#       another name;
#   (b) asymmetry of the errors under uncertainty about one's own
#       behavior. Nobody knows in advance how they will sit through a
#       2011-2019 flat stretch in a trend sleeve. A trend leg too small
#       costs a few basis points of missed diversification, invisible in
#       the floor metrics; a trend leg too big, abandoned at the bottom,
#       costs a permanent capitulation on a third of the book at the worst
#       possible moment. Stand where being wrong is cheap: 20.
#   REVISION PROTOCOL: revisable UPWARD on lived evidence only (keep a
#   decision journal through each flat or losing stretch), 20 -> 25 at an
#   annual review only, NEVER during a drawdown of the sleeve, and no cut
#   during a drawdown either. Mitigations: judge trend only at the whole
#   book level, never the line alone; look quarterly, not daily. Base
#   rate: Morningstar's "Mind the Gap" measures ~1-2%/yr lost to timing by
#   investors who all thought they were steady.
#   QUANTIFIED: a decumulation Monte-Carlo sweep of the trend
#   weight over {7.5, 12, 17, 22} improves the floor metrics MONOTONICALLY
#   up to 22 in five of six return models (years at the floor in a lost
#   decade: 6.8 -> 2.8 going from 7.5 to 17); the only model that pays is
#   the flat-CTA decade, and only its median (-0.24 pt/yr), never its
#   tail. The regret asymmetry argued above is measured, not just pleaded,
#   and the upward-only revision protocol has numbers behind it. A trend
#   dose of 7.5 (the blind liability-driven designer's) leaves ~2 years
#   at the floor on the table in the bad worlds.
#   VOLATILITY TARGETING OF THE EQUITY SLEEVE: measured, not adopted. The
#   central rule (12% target, 3-month daily volatility, floor 0.5, no
#   leverage, switches made inside the shelters, hence tax-free) cuts the
#   book's worst drawdown from -16% to -7% over 1996-2026 (Sharpe 0.98 ->
#   1.05, -0.5 pt/yr of CAGR), but the gain vanishes over 1987-2026, where
#   an equal-exposure control without timing does better. The benefit
#   rests on two episodes (2000-2003, 2007-2009: slow declines preceded by
#   rising volatility); crashes from a calm market (1987, 1990, 2020) and
#   the rebounds are missed. One variant of thirteen beats its control on
#   all three windows (monthly 6-month volatility at 12%): a hypothesis to
#   retest, not a result. Capped leverage (1.25 or 1.5) buys a little CAGR
#   but never Sharpe once costs are paid, and it is already on when a
#   crash starts from calm. Re-measure if the trend dose is ever cut: the
#   two partly do the same job.
#
# ## 5. The bond tent that does not say its name
#
#   42 points of equity at the start is not "too defensive": the idea is
#   the rising equity glidepath (Pfau & Kitces, Journal of Financial
#   Planning, January 2014) taken deliberately inside the retirement red
#   zone (~5 years before, ~10 after the exit). The defensive start is a
#   TRAJECTORY, not a state. But the rule used here is NOT the glidepath
#   the paper tests: the paper raises the equity share linearly on a
#   schedule and rebalances to that target; the rule here (past year ~8,
#   stop refilling the linkers and never rebalance equity DOWN) lets the
#   equity share drift up when markets and the spent linkers allow it,
#   with no schedule and no cap, and lifts nothing after a long decline.
#   It is a variant inspired by that literature, to be measured against a
#   static target and against a scheduled glidepath on the same paths
#   before it is written into the household's rule page (target path,
#   buying equity on the way down or not, priority over the bands). The
#   intuition stands: the return given up is concentrated on the years
#   where it buys the most survival. That a static comparison understates
#   this book is a hypothesis to test, and a higher mean return would not
#   prove a better tail.
#   GUARDRAIL PLUMBING that goes with the tent: if the plan includes an
#   indexed income arriving mid-retirement (a state pension), REBASE the
#   guardrail reference rate at each income step (the exit itself, the
#   end of a bridge income, the first pension). Without the rebase, the
#   pension's arrival mechanically crushes the measured withdrawal rate
#   and a prosperity rule would raise spending for the wrong reason. That
#   holds for a rate measured on the NET withdrawal. By default the
#   `pofo -fire` engine measures it on TOTAL spending, where a rebase has
#   no object; its gkNet switch reads the net rate and re-centres the band
#   at each income step. Pick one definition before writing the rule
#   down (s. 12 compares them).
#   The same tent seen from the liability: when the pension arrives the
#   net liability drops sharply, which frees part of the base for equity.
#   The upward drift past year 8 is not only a trajectory choice; it
#   falls out of the balance sheet.
#   Name the mechanical loss honestly: against a build centered on a
#   stacked 90/60 core, notional exposure falls from ~130% to ~116% (the
#   convention of s. 8), because cheap embedded leverage was handed back
#   when the equity moved into the shelters. If the floor metrics ever
#   suffer for it, recover leverage at the margin with +1-2 points of long
#   duration (duration is cheap leverage), never with more equity.
#
# ## 6. The deflation leg: two issuers, the dollar left unhedged
#
#   The bond overlay lost with the stacked core is rebuilt by TWO long
#   duration lines, half and half, so the whole leg does not sit on one
#   sovereign complex. This is the only place in the book where the dollar
#   is an asset rather than a risk:
#   1. Two engines add up. In a growth crisis the dollar rises (haven) AND
#      Treasuries rise (flight to quality); a euro holder collects both.
#      2008 measured: about +26% for the unhedged US line against about
#      +7% for euro 25+. The euro sovereign drags its spread risk in
#      exactly when it is needed (2011 showed it again).
#   2. Hedging keeps only half of the engine: the forward swaps the US
#      short rate for the euro short rate, the duration and the long US
#      curve stay American, but the haven dollar of point 1 is gone. An
#      accumulating EUR-hedged class exists (iShares $ Treasury 20+ EUR
#      Hedged Acc, DTEH, listed since 2026-03, 0.10%): holding the
#      unhedged line is a risk choice, not a lack of product.
#   3. The currency risk taken in exchange is small and BOUNDED: 5 points
#      of dollar against an all-euro floor (linkers, euro long duration,
#      the cash buffer, the hedged trend leg).
#   CAVEAT, written for the future reader: the "crisis means USD and
#   Treasuries rally together" playbook assumes a crisis NOT centered on
#   the United States. The 2025 tariff episodes showed the dollar and long
#   Treasuries falling in concert when the shock is US fiscal and
#   institutional: the line can then lose on both legs at once. That is
#   precisely why it is capped at 5 and never averaged up. And 2025 was
#   not the first failure: in the 1990
#   oil-shock recession (US-centered: S&L, recession, dollar sliding
#   against the mark) the unhedged US line printed -6.7% in EUR while the
#   euro long line printed +5.9%. Both times, the half that paid was the
#   liability-currency half. The two-issuer split is not diversification
#   cosmetics; it is what has actually carried the leg through both of
#   its measured failures.
#   Accepted corollary: outside crises the line moves with EURUSD,
#   visible volatility with no expected return attached. That is the
#   premium on an insurance that pays twice when it pays.
#   THE SHAPE OF THE DURATION, examined three times, unchanged:
#   - Intermediate multi-currency duration through a stacked 90/60 core
#     (the blind from-scratch book's choice): in an isolated test it beats
#     a lone euro 25+ line in 2022 (-9.1% against -10.9% real); at the
#     whole-book level, with gold and trend opposite, this book does better
#     in 2022 and dominates on all six models. What helped in the isolated
#     test was the PROFILE (intermediate, multi-currency), not the leverage.
#   - The gold cut from 15 to 12 moves the book's 2022 by about -0.3 point
#     real; sending the three freed points to euro 25+ would have moved it
#     by -1.6: the long pair stays at 10 points.
#   - A MIX OF MATURITIES (a manager's argument: the short end follows
#     policy rates, the long end long-run expectations, so they answer
#     different regimes), measured at the whole-book level on three
#     windows: per UNIT of duration, 7-10y loses less in a rate shock and
#     gains more in a growth shock. But the leg is constrained in CAPITAL
#     (10 points, no leverage), the long pair carries 2.3 times the
#     duration of a 7-10y pair, and that quantity is what pays in the
#     deflation crash the leg exists for (2008: +24% for the leg against
#     +16%). At the book level 7-10y moves CAGR by only 0.06 to 0.13 pt/yr
#     and the worst 12 months by +-0.5 pt, in both directions by window: no
#     steering metric moves. A barbell is dominated everywhere; an
#     equal-duration mix needs 25+ STRIPS a French retail holder cannot
#     buy. The long pair's price remains 2022-2023 (+1.1% of book against
#     +4.6% with 7-10y), paid by gold and trend.
#   The long pair is defensible only BECAUSE gold and trend absorb the
#   rate shock: reopen the shape (variant: euro 7-10y in place of euro
#   25+, the US line kept) only if one of those legs is cut or the leg's
#   role changes, never on the level of rates.
#
# ## 7. Gold at 12: the dose follows the price of the insurance
#
#   WHY NOT A TOKEN DOSE. Cutting gold to a minimum ("no premium, minimum
#   dose") while keeping long duration for its hedging demand applies the
#   same logic selectively. The regime gold covers (long stagflation,
#   monetary debasement) is the worst-covered regime in the whole book
#   (Neville, Draaisma, Funnell, Harvey & van Hemert, JPM 2021): long
#   duration LOSES there (2022), and trend is slow to turn and is exactly
#   the sleeve capped for behavioral reasons above. Gold and trend are the
#   only two legs of that regime; weakening both at once is incoherent.
#   The dose is what the regime demands GIVEN that the other leg is
#   capped, and 10 is its floor (s. 10).
#   HEAD-TO-HEAD CHECK (gold then at 15): a leaner variant at 9
#   points of gold was dominated by this file across six decumulation
#   return models, INCLUDING one where every sleeve is capped at prudent
#   forward priors (gold at 1%/yr real). Gold is paid by its covariance
#   with bad states, not by its 2005-2025 tailwind (~9%/yr real, not to be
#   extrapolated). An isolated weight sweep cannot see this (8 vs 11 reads
#   as noise); only the full head-to-head, in the tails, does.
#   WHY 12 RATHER THAN 15. An insurance has a price, and the dose follows
#   it. Four measurements (2026-09), none of them a forecast:
#   1. THE REAL PRICE. Gold deflated by US CPI stood in 2026-09 at 3.97
#      times its 1968-2026 median (4.46 in euros on French CPI), against
#      2.6 at the 1980 peak and 2.5 in 2011. The evidence that a high real
#      price announces a low ten-year return (Erb & Harvey 2013; "Is There
#      Still a Golden Dilemma?", 2024) is WEAK: two independent episodes,
#      and a counter-example running since 2020 (+9%/yr real from a start
#      at 2.35 times). A reason to stop adding, not a reason to sell.
#   2. THE RISK SHARE. At 15 points gold carried about 40% of the book's
#      variance over the twelve months to 2026-09 (s. 4).
#   3. THE PAYOFF BY STARTING PRICE, real returns in EUR. In the stress
#      episodes gold entered below 1.5 times its median, it paid: +71%
#      over 1973-74, +33% from 2007-11 to 2009-02. In those it entered
#      above 2, it mostly did not: -46% from 1980-02 to 1982-06, -31% in
#      2013, -1% in 2022 (the inflation shock itself), -18% from 2026-02
#      to 2026-06. Exceptions: the 2011 euro crisis (+15%) and the 2025
#      tariff shock (+7%). A small sample, one sign.
#   4. THE CARRY. Gold hedges NEGATIVE REAL RATES rather than inflation as
#      such (1980-82, 2013 and 2022 are the three times real yields rose
#      against it). With ten-year real yields near 2.8% in dollars in
#      2026-09, holding a zero-yield asset had not cost as much in twenty
#      years.
#   WHAT THE SIMULATIONS CAN AND CANNOT SAY. A decumulation Monte-Carlo
#   that lowers gold's mean and keeps its co-movements finds the low dose
#   better by construction, so it proves little. Read as a break-even it
#   is more useful: in the tail (5th-percentile terminal wealth), 15
#   equals 10 when gold earns about +1%/yr real on the 2005-2025
#   co-movements, 0% on 1972-2025, -1% on 1987-2025; above, 15 wins,
#   below, 10. The stake is a few percent of terminal wealth either way,
#   and history adds a nuance: when gold gave back its premium (-83% real
#   over 1980-2001) the rest of the book was thriving, so a 1980 cohort
#   replayed on a representative schedule never cut its spending, at any
#   dose. With equity as dear as it was in
#   2026 that comfort cannot be assumed twice, and the one scenario where
#   the dose really bites is equity AND gold deflating together, against
#   15. Hence a small move, inside the range, made by NOT BUYING the last
#   points rather than by selling.
#   THE ONE-OFF IN THE 1970s RECORD. Part of what gold did over 1972-1980
#   was the release of a price administered at $35 since 1934, a spring
#   that cannot be compressed again (s. 12). Taking it out (gold
#   recentred to a zero real return inside the high-inflation regime, its
#   co-movements kept) roughly halves the edge of 15 over 10 in that
#   regime without cancelling it: ruin 1.9% against 2.6%, equal floor
#   years. What remains is covariance; dose on it, not on the level gain.
#   RULE. Target 12, band 9 to 15: sell back to 12 above 15 points, buy
#   only below 9. Go back toward 15 if the real price returns under about
#   three times its median with volatility back near its long-run level,
#   or if real yields turn negative again. No dose indexed on the price
#   itself: replayed in real time since 1972 (expanding median), such a
#   rule moves neither the return nor the drawdown, and bands do the same
#   work without a median to estimate.
#   BEHAVIORAL GUARDRAIL: gold stood 20% below its peak the day the dose
#   was cut, and rebalancing to 15 would have bought. The cut stands
#   because it rests on the real price and the risk share (20% off its
#   peak, gold was still at four times its median) and is written with its
#   way back. Never read it as a precedent for cutting a dose in a slump.
#   WHAT GOLD DOES NOT COVER (1990, measured on the deep-history stand-in
#   variant, s. 12): gold is a DOLLAR asset, and in an inflation shock
#   where the dollar falls it can lose double digits for a euro holder.
#   Through the 1990 oil-spike recession, the worst drawdown of the whole
#   forty-year test, gold printed -13.0% in EUR (only -3.1% in USD; the
#   rest is the dollar) while hedged trend printed +16.0% and was the one
#   leg that paid. Read the division of labor precisely: TREND catches the
#   shock as it happens (long energy, short bonds: +16% in 1990, +15% in
#   2022 for the net index); GOLD covers the monetary aftermath and the
#   debasement decade, on its own clock. Judging gold on the shock year
#   is the same mistake as judging the linkers on 2022.

# ## 11. Blind spots, stated rather than discovered later
#
#   NO LONG VOLATILITY, the dragon's fifth leg. A crash that completes in
#     days (February 2018, March 2020) is covered by nothing here: trend
#     needs weeks to reposition, gold is unreliable in the first sessions,
#     duration only helps if the shock is deflationary. Accepted because
#     listed long-vol vehicles bleed structurally and a household cannot
#     run an option book with discipline. MEASURED as well: a 3-point
#     long-vol sleeve was simulated in the
#     decumulation Monte-Carlo; near-neutral under generous assumptions
#     (-4%/yr bleed, +90% payoff on a <=-30% equity year), NEGATIVE under
#     a realistic one (-10%/yr bleed, +60%). And the retail UCITS shelf
#     was surveyed: the listed VIX-roll product lost ~99% since 2012, the
#     genuine tail funds are institutional-access. Trend buys roughly
#     three times the protection at positive carry. The crash in days is
#     carried by the buffer (s. 14).
#   NO BROAD COMMODITIES beyond gold: an energy shock is reached only
#     through the trend sleeve, which has to catch it first. MEASURED as
#     well: a 2-point broad-commodity sleeve was tested head-to-head
#     and dragged every model (-2.2%/yr real over 2005-2025, and a prudent
#     prior only caps upside tailwinds, it never lifts a drag).
#   NO CAT BONDS, NO AAA CLOs: the retail UCITS shelf offers nothing
#     accessible at a sane cost. Closed.
#   THE EQUITY SLEEVE IS A US MEGA-CAP BET in disguise (world cap weight),
#     and small value is only a partial answer to it.
#   CONCENTRATION IN NAMES, not just in assets: two managers carry the
#     whole trend sleeve, one of them priced at NAV rather than traded,
#     and the sheltered world fund is swap based. Counterparty and manager
#     risk are the price of the wrapper and of the diversification.
#   TAXES ARE OUTSIDE THE BACKTEST entirely, while they are half the
#     argument for where each line is held (s. 0).
#   THE FLOOR IS IN EUROS and much of the equity is not: a long EUR
#     appreciation is a real drag that no line here offsets.
#   CONSIDERED AND REJECTED: all-maturity linkers (a real-rate bet, not an
#     inflation hedge), EUR-hedged long Treasuries (the US leg is held for
#     the crisis dollar, which the hedge removes, s. 6), keeping a stacked 90/60 core in
#     the taxable account (restores leverage but wastes the shelters), a
#     single trend manager (manager risk on the least certain premium),
#     cash instead of linkers (see section 1).
#   CONSIDERED, MEASURED AND REJECTED (on the long-window stand-in
#     variants of s. 12): a broad-commodity sleeve, 4 points
#     carved out of gold as a standing oil-shock hedge. Over 1996-2026 it

#     drops the worst rolling 5y from 0.80% to 0.28%, for about +0.6
#     points in 2022: a premium paid every year for a hedge that pays once

#     trends (+16% in 1990, +15% in 2022 for the net index). An oil shock

#     common window. A US TIPS 1-10 stand-in is not a substitute: US CPI
#     base, different series.
#   THE DIRECT TREND SHARE CLASS is chosen on fee PLUMBING, not on past
#     returns, and the reasoning generalises to any multi-class fund
#     (details in pofo docs/specs/aqr-mf.txt). Every EUR class of that fund
#     holds the same portfolio, so comparing two of them on NAV measures
#     their fee difference and never the manager. What each levies INSIDE
#     its NAV: 0.23%/yr for the cheapest class, 0.78% plus 10% of the
#     gains above cash for the performance-fee one, 1.26% flat for the one
#     held here. The flat class wins on two grounds. Its whole cost sits
#     in the quoted price, so the backtest above is what a holder gets.
#     And a performance fee bills the most in the years trend explodes,
#     which are the crash years this sleeve exists to pay the floor
#     through: in a year at +40% before fees, the performance-fee class
#     takes ~4.5 points (0.78 of base fee plus ~3.7 of performance fee
#     above cash) against 1.26 for the flat one. The cheapest
#     class is cheapest only because its management fee is WAIVED at the
#     manager's sole discretion, revocable without notice, and leaving
#     after a revocation realises a taxable gain. Break-even between the
#     flat and performance-fee classes: an excess of 4.8%/yr over cash.
#   WEIGHTS ARE INTEGERS on purpose. Half a point is worth less than the
#     drift between two rebalancings, and far less than the width of the
#     sane ranges. They are rounded off a design logic, never optimized
#     (DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal, RFS 2009: twenty years of data do not
#     estimate means well enough to beat equal weighting out of sample).
#
# ## 13. Running rules
#
#   - Rebalance on bands, executed in the taxable account; spending IS
#     rebalancing. Inside the shelters the only trades available are
#     equity for equity and, in the PEE, a tax-free switch to its
#     money-market fund (s. 0), so no move between the book's sleeves
#     happens there.
#   - Always use limit orders on the thin LISTED lines (linkers, the
#     replication trend ETF, euro 25+). The direct trend fund is not
#     listed at all: it deals at the day's NAV on an early-afternoon
#     cut-off and settles three days later, so there is no price to
#     limit, and a rebalance that pairs the two trend legs has to budget
#     for the one-day lag between them.
#   - One management day per quarter: withdrawal, bands, journal.
#   - Annual review: trend 20 -> 25 on lived evidence only (section 4);
#     the unhedged US leg never above 5 (section 6); the total expense and
#     fund range of each shelter; re-run the withdrawal simulation with
#     current capital and age. Also check that BOTH trend managers still
#     trade energy and commodities, not only financials: in the oil-shock
#     regime the trend sleeve is the only leg that answers (1990, 2022),
#     and a CTA that has drifted to purely financial futures silently
#     vacates that post. The replication leg stays in its UNHEDGED class
#     (s. 8): reopen that only past ~30 points of total dollar exposure,
#     never on a move in rates.
#   - A YOUNG OR SMALL SHARE CLASS (the direct trend class today): the
#     fund deals at NAV, so there is no spread to speak of. The real risk
#     is the CLASS CLOSING, and it forks. Closed by MERGER into a sister
#     class of the same sub-fund, it should benefit from the tax deferral
#     French law grants fund mergers (art. 150-0 B CGI, cost basis kept;
#     confirm in the BOFiP when it happens). Closed by FORCED REDEMPTION,
#     it is a taxable sale at 31.4% on the accrued gain, plus days out of
#     the market and a repurchase elsewhere. The expected cost therefore
#     scales with the latent gain at closure, hence an ASYMMETRY: at the
#     INITIAL purchase (gain starting at zero; if the class dies within
#     1-2 years by forced redemption, the tax falls on 1-2 years of gain
#     at most) a small class is ACCEPTABLE; an assets threshold of 50-100 M only
#     makes sense for a SWITCH out of a line already held, which would
#     crystallize its gains as well. What must prove itself is the CLASS,
#     not the sub-fund: check its size, and check that the broker really
#     serves it to a French resident.
#   - Duration vulnerability is read off the STARTING long yield, not
#     rebalanced away: at 8-11% coupons the 1990 rate shock left euro long
#     bonds positive; from ~1% carry the same shock in 2022 cost -38%.
#     The current euro long yield is the gauge of how bad the next 1994
#     would be; it changes what to expect, never the weights.
#   - Steering metrics: years spent at the floor, median year of the first
#     cut, median terminal wealth, real wealth P5 at ~90 (s. 12). NOT the
#     Sharpe of a 20-year backtest: its second decimal is estimation
#     noise, and a pre-tax backtest is structurally blind to the wrapper
#     arbitrage this design rests on (18.6% deferred against 31.4%).
#   - Rebase the guardrail reference at each income step (s. 5).
#   - Test the progressive-scale option against the flat tax EVERY year
#     of decumulation: at an 11% marginal bracket, scale plus social
#     levies minus the deductible CSG is ~28.9% all-in against 31.4%.
#   - Harvest realized losses in the taxable account each December (ten
#     year carry-forward in France): on a book of 7+ decorrelated lines
#     there is almost always a loser to purge, worth ~0.15-0.25%/yr.
#   - SURVIVOR FALLBACK, written in advance: a two-page notice plus a
#     three-line replacement book (35 world equity / 45 euro linkers 1-10
#     / 20 gold, rebalanced once a year), switched AUTOMATICALLY if the
#     managing spouse dies, no waiting for a good market moment. Measured
#     cost of the simplicity: ~-0.2 pt/yr real and the loss of the
#     conditional hedges (a 2022 at ~-14% real instead of ~-9). A design
#     nobody can run when its designer is gone is a design flaw.
#   - DEFERRED LIFE ANNUITY decision point: mandatory review at ~75, buy
#     if capital has drifted more than ~25% below its median trajectory.
#     Late annuitization is the cheapest longevity insurance for the
#     beyond-the-horizon tail: mortality credits become significant, and
#     French taxation of purchased annuities improves sharply with age at
#     purchase (30% of the annuity taxable when bought after 70).
#   - REGISTER OF FOREGONE RETURN, against the unease of being "nibbled":
#     every return given up is entered here with what it buys, so the
#     total is seen rather than felt. Deliberate ones, pre-tax:
#       the PEE fund charge on 23 points, ~-0.14%/yr: buys the 18.6 vs
#         31.4 tax wedge plus the float of s. 0 (+10 to +22% of net wealth
#         on that tranche over 20 years in the worked example, ~0.5 to
#         1%/yr of growth in the ratio of the two wealths, not points of
#         CAGR for the whole book);
#       giving up the stacked core, -0.07 to -0.14%/yr of notional handed
#         back (130 -> 116), the intermediate multi-currency duration
#         profile that won the isolated test (s. 6), and the attachment
#         to a familiar design (a real behavioral cost: a book one likes
#         is held better). Against it: the tax wedge (a stacked core fits
#         no shelter), a HIGHER equivalent duration in exchange (s. 8),
#         and a head-to-head this book wins BECAUSE gold and trend absorb
#         what the intermediate profile absorbed.
#     Total deliberate: ~0.3-0.4%/yr pre-tax. Identified against it: loss
#     harvesting +0.15-0.25, the cheaper Europe small-value class +0.05,
#     the progressive scale in low-bracket years (~2.5 points of tax),
#     and the PEE wedge. NET CLEARLY POSITIVE: cents pre-tax against tens
#     of cents after tax and in the tail. Two reading rules: (a) "losses"
#     measured against vehicles a French retail holder cannot buy are not
#     decisions but the price already paid for the setup, never counted
#     twice; (b) the item that really eats return is behavioral (Mind the
#     Gap, 1-2%/yr): one capitulation costs more than ten years of all the
#     items above, and avoiding it is what they buy.
#     RULE: a new sacrifice is accepted only if it improves a measured
#     steering metric OR its net-of-tax gain exceeds its pre-tax cost; the
#     DISCRETIONARY budget is capped at ~0.5%/yr pre-tax, beyond which
#     something is removed before anything is added.
#
# ## 14. The buffer held outside the weights
#
#   The weights cover the invested book. Two reserves live apart, and
#   neither is ever counted in the weights or in the solvency of any line
#   above:
#   THE BUCKET, three years of spending (total spending, or spending net
#     of a bridge income: a choice to make; the simulations of s. 12 use
#     total spending). With a
#     PEE, its money-market fund is the natural home: the switch in is
#     tax-free, the refill is a sale of the PEE world fund in good years
#     (the first draw of s. 0), and the deferred tax beats a money-market
#     ETF in the taxable account. Without one: a euro overnight-rate ETF
#     (XEON, LU0290358497) for the fast half, and later the
#     capital-guaranteed euro fund of a French life-insurance contract,
#     zero unit-linked, for the slow half. That contract's gains pay 17.2%
#     of social levies (life insurance was left out of the 2026 rise to
#     18.6%), and after eight years a couple's yearly allowance (EUR 9,200
#     of gains free of income tax) applies; that allowance is worth at most
#     ~EUR 1,300 a year over the taxable account, which a 0.5%/yr
#     unit-linked charge consumes past ~EUR 250,000 of units: hence a PURE
#     euro fund and a SMALL contract.
#   THE PRECAUTION: regulated savings accounts (Livret A, LDDS), outside
#     the market and outside the plan.
#   Rules: the bucket is drawn only when the book is in a real drawdown
#   deeper than 10%, read on the MARKET: a flow-neutral (time-weighted)
#   real return index of the growth sleeve from its peak (measured on the
#   capital stock, planned spending-down alone would set it off). Refilled as a priority once the book is back within 5% of its peak.
#   It is what carries the crash that completes in days (s. 11), which no
#   line of the book covers.
#   Considered and REJECTED: replacing part of the bucket by a contingent
#   lombard credit line. It is revocable, its capacity is correlated with
#   the very crisis it would serve, and its rate floats. Free optionality
#   is acceptable as a second line if it costs nothing; never a
#   substitute.
#
# ## 15. Forward readings: each scenario lands on a written lever
#
#   Forecasts cannot add a line; their one use is to check that no
#   plausible scenario demands a lever this file does not already hold.
#   Checked, scenario by scenario, each mapped onto the regime map of
#   s. 9 and onto its measured anchor. The "expected" losses below are
#   rough arithmetic, illustrations rather than measured bounds, and the
#   responses of gold, trend and the euro long line are assumptions drawn
#   from the anchors, not certain payments:
#   A. AN AI-BUBBLE BUST (a slow deflationary crash). Anchor: 2000-02,
#      +2.5% cumulative against -44.5% for world equity (s. 12). The
#      analogue is structurally sound: the same valuation gap between
#      mega-cap growth and value as in 2000, and the 14 points of small
#      value are the SPECIFIC asset of the rotation that followed. The
#      2026 twist: if inflation stays sticky (tariffs, energy capex) the
#      Fed cuts little and the scenario takes the shape of 2022, not of
#      2001: duration helps less, trend and gold carry. Expected: flat to
#      -10% real. Levers: small value, trend, bands.
#   B. A DOLLAR DEVALUATION WITH RISING US RATES ("sell America"). Anchors:
#      2025 (tariffs) and 1990 (the US 20+ line -6.7% in EUR). Gross
#      dollar sensitivity ~35-40 points (5 US duration, ~10 replication
#      trend, ~20 of US equity, part of small value): a 20% devaluation is
#      ~-7 points of translation plus the de-rating. Against it: gold IS
#      the asset of monetary distrust, the euro long line collects the
#      flight to the Bund, and short dollar is the cleanest trend trade
#      there is (a slow, trending scenario by nature). Expected: -5 to -10%
#      real. This is the scenario that weighs most on the currency leg of
#      the trend sleeve (s. 8), and the only one that could one day reopen
#      the hedged class; it is read on the book's TOTAL effective dollar
#      exposure (~16 points measured), never on the rate gap.
#   C. A AND B TOGETHER (US bust, a Fed unable to cut, a falling dollar).
#      The true worst corner. Rough arithmetic: equity -40% locally and
#      -15% of currency on ~40 points is ~-20 points; against it gold +4,
#      trend +3-5, the euro long line +1, linkers stable. Net -13 to -19%
#      real: THE ORDER OF THE MEASURED 1990 (-20.7%, pessimistic, s. 12).
#      The tent is sized for it: bucket for years 1-3 plus linkers for
#      the following years (4 to ~9-12 depending on the draw, s. 2) means
#      no forced equity sale for most of a decade, provided the linker
#      fund, which has no maturity, does not fall in the shock itself.
#      No new lever needed.
#   D. AN ENERGY SHOCK (wars plus the power demand of AI). Anchors: 2022
#      (book ~-2%) without a weak dollar, 1990 with one. No physical
#      commodities (measured and rejected, s. 11): the regime rests on the
#      linkers (they accrue the HICP by contract, energy included) and on
#      trend, which RENTS the exposure if it lasts months. A two-week
#      spike that reverses is caught by nobody, and it reverses (WTI in
#      1990: +112%, then -29%).
#   The scenarios missing from the intuitive list, the first being the
#   most important:
#   E. THE MELT-UP. The logical sequel of "a soft market is hard to
#      imagine" is 1998-99, not the crash: three years of AI at +40%/yr
#      with this book trailing by 12-24 points a year (1997-99 measured:
#      +58% against +115% cumulative, s. 12). Nobody sells this book at
#      the bottom of a crash; it gets sold at the top of a mania, out of
#      boredom. RISK NUMBER ONE, behavioral, and the sentence is already
#      written (s. 4; s. 12, point 3).
#   F. A FRENCH OR EURO DEBT CRISIS. The only scenario that attacks the
#      FLOOR itself (the linkers and the euro long line are France- and
#      Italy-heavy). Measured kit, 2011: US 20+ +37.1% in EUR, gold +13.1,
#      euro 25+ +7.6 all the same. The mirror justification of the dollar
#      leg, and the scenario where its cap at 5 has a COST, accepted with
#      open eyes.
#   G. FINANCIAL REPRESSION (inflation at 4-6%, rates held below it, for
#      a decade, no crash). The historical way out of public debt at 110%
#      of GDP, hence likely. It is the scenario where the TAX LEAK on
#      indexation (s. 1) becomes the central subject, and the one that
#      most justifies the wrapper architecture (s. 0). Cushions listed in
#      s. 1; gold and trend carry the real return.
#   H. THE SOFT MARKET ruled out by assumption. A 2011-2019 (trend in the
#      desert, equities lukewarm): ~3-4%/yr real, no disaster, but the
#      regime where the temptation to cut trend peaks; the protocol of
#      s. 4 is written for it. Leaving it out of one's forecasts is
#      exactly how it hurts.
#   CONCLUSION, to reread before any urge to "position" the book: no
#   scenario listed demands a new line or a different weight, under the
#   assumed response of each leg; each lands on a lever already written.
#   A design result conditional on this list, not a bound, and not an
#   evasion either: the book exists to make forecasting unnecessary. The uncovered corner
#   remains the crash in days (s. 11), and the buffer carries it (s. 14).
#
# ## 16. The trend sleeve: the panel, and why these two lines
#
#   IN ONE SENTENCE: THE DIRECT MANAGER PAID THE RECENT ACCIDENT MONTHS,
#   THE REPLICATION LINE HOLDS THE LONG REGIME. Two managers, two hedging
#   horizons, both measured on this book: that is the 50/50, and why
#   neither line replaces the other.
#   JUDGMENT RULE, valid for any future candidate: MEASURING A SLEEVE
#   AGAINST ITSELF IS NOT MEASURING IT AGAINST THE BOOK. A ranking built
#   on the pair with the replication line alone names a different winner
#   from the one the whole book names, where the other sleeves already do
#   part of the work. The test is behavior in THE BOOK'S WORST MONTHS.
#   And the criterion that matters is not the correlation to equities:
#   the book's 25 worst months are NOT equity crashes (2013-06, 2015, the
#   2022 rate shock, the 2025-03 dollar). The right test is a candidate's
#   mean return over the decile of months where THE BOOK bleeds, and its
#   hit rate there. The table runs on the strictly common window
#   2020-07 to 2026-08 (74 months, the only one where the whole panel
#   exists): bottom decile = the book's 7 worst months (2022-05, -06,
#   -08, -09, -12, 2025-03, 2026-03), hit rate = share of those 7 months
#   where the candidate is positive (6 of 7 for AQR):
#     candidate                  bottom decile   hit rate   quintile
#     AQR MF (= the class held)  +2.44 %/month      86%      +1.09
#     AlphaSimplex               +1.52              43%      -0.79
#     Winton                     +1.27              71%      +0.00
#     Campbell                   +1.19              57%      +0.79
#     Metori Epsilon             +0.95              43%      -0.62

#     Quantica                   +0.43              57%      -1.20
#     the book itself            -2.81               .       -1.89
#   AQR ranks first on three of the five axes tested (decile, quintile,
#   quintile hit rate), and by far: +2.44% in the month the book loses
