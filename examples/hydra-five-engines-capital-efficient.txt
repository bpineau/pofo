# Hydra (five engines, capital-efficient) -- the frontier build.
#
# STATUS: research portfolio, NOT a buy list. Two engines live in US-listed
#   funds with no PRIIPs KID (GDE, ZROZ), so an EU retail investor cannot
#   implement this today. It exists (a) to mark where the risk/return
#   frontier of this engine philosophy actually sits, (b) as the canonical
#   rationale file for the hydra family, and (c) as a WATCH LIST: if a UCITS
#   twin of the missing pieces ever launches, this is the build it unlocks.
#   The implementable version is hydra-five-engines-ucits.txt, which gives
#   up ~8 points of notional, and 1.2 points of measured CAGR, to stay
#   buyable.
#
# Thesis in one line: five engines, and every sleeve that CAN be expressed
#   through an embedded-leverage instrument is, so ~125% of notional
#   exposure fits in 100% of capital with no margin account, no borrow rate
#   spread, and no daily-reset decay.
#
# ## What 100 EUR actually holds (the exposure ledger)
#
#   18 NTSG   -> 16.2 world equity + 10.8 sovereign futures (~7y duration)
#   12 ZPRV   -> 12   US small-cap value equity
#   20 MFEH   -> 20   managed-futures trend (replication, EUR-hedged)
#   20 RAEF   -> 20   managed-futures trend (direct TSMOM, EUR-hedged)
#   20 GDE    -> 18   US large-cap equity + 18 gold futures
#   10 ZROZ   -> 10   US Treasuries at ~27y duration
#   ------------------------------------------------------------------
#   Total     ~46 equity + ~21 bonds + ~40 trend + ~18 gold  = ~125%
#
#   The extra ~25 points are financed inside NTSG and GDE at roughly the
#   T-bill rate (futures carry), the cheapest leverage a retail investor
#   can reach and the whole point of the build: the diversifiers stack ON
#   TOP of a full growth engine instead of being funded by shrinking it.
#   The trend budget is the exception and is deliberately unstacked: see
#   the RSBT entry in the rejected-alternatives ledger.
#
# ## Why embedded leverage, not external leverage
#   The same ~125% book could be assembled from plain funds plus external
#   financing: a margin/Lombard loan on the CTO, short box spreads, or a
#   DIY futures overlay (a levered variant of the buyable hydra was
#   backtested at the cash rate + 1%: it works on paper, with all the
#   fragilities below). Embedded stacking wins on five fronts:
#   1. NO MARGIN CALL, EVER. External leverage is path-dependent: a deep
#      drawdown raises the account's leverage ratio exactly when prices
#      are lowest, and the broker (whose risk desk can also RAISE margin
#      requirements overnight mid-crisis) forces sales at the bottom. A
#      90/60 fund rebalances its own overlay internally; its NAV can
#      drop, but the holder can never be liquidated out of the position.
#      For a decumulation portfolio this is the decisive property:
#      forced deleveraging at the low is sequence risk squared.
#   2. LEVERAGE RATIO BEHAVIOR. A loan is a FIXED liability against a
#      falling asset: leverage drifts UP in drawdowns (anti-convex, the
#      dangerous direction) unless the investor actively deleverages at
#      the worst moment. The funds keep notional ratios roughly constant
#      by design, without the daily-reset volatility decay of 2x/3x ETFs.
#   3. FINANCING RATE. Futures carry prices at ~T-bill, the wholesale
#      rate. Retail margin runs benchmark +1 to +3%; only disciplined
#      box-spread selling gets near ~+0.2-0.5%, and it must be re-rolled
#      at maturity at whatever the new rate is, with options spreads and
#      portfolio-margin requirements on top.
#   4. TAX (French CTO). Margin interest is NOT deductible against PFU-
#      taxed gains: a pure after-tax dead loss. A box spread's implicit
#      interest at least realizes as an offsettable capital loss. Inside
#      a fund, financing nets against returns BEFORE NAV, i.e. fully
#      pre-tax, automatically. Embedded > box > margin, tax-wise.
#   5. BALANCE SHEET AND OPERATIONS. Fund leverage is invisible to the
#      investor's personal balance sheet: no debt line eating mortgage
#      capacity (taux d'endettement), no rolls to execute, no expiries
#      to diarize, nothing to mis-execute in a volatile week. The fund
#      does daily, at institutional cost, what a retail investor does
#      badly quarterly.
#   Honest counterpoints: a well-run box-spread program costs about what
#   the stacked funds' TER premium does, so the case rests on points 1, 2,
#   4 and 5, not on raw cost; options and daily-reset leveraged ETFs DO
#   charge an embedded-leverage premium (Frazzini & Pedersen, "Embedded
#   Leverage", 2022), which is why only futures-overlay funds qualify for
#   this build; and external leverage can lever ANY mix, including
#   UCITS-only sleeves, while stacking only exists in the combinations
#   issuers ship -- which is exactly why this file doubles as a watch list.
#
# ## Why engines, and why these mechanics
#
# 1. DIVERSIFY ACROSS RETURN SOURCES. Combining lowly-correlated streams
#    that each carry positive expected return is what lifts risk-adjusted
#    return, far more than adding names does (Ilmanen & Kizer, "The Death
#    of Diversification Has Been Greatly Exaggerated", JPM 2012;
#    Choueifaty & Coignard, "Toward Maximum Diversification", JPM 2008).
#    Each head is a distinct, documented return source driven by a
#    different macro force, so their worst years rarely coincide.
# 2. SIZE BY RISK CONTRIBUTION, NOT CAPITAL (Qian, "Risk Parity
#    Portfolios", PanAgora 2005; Bridgewater's All Weather): balance
#    exposures across growth/inflation/deflation outcomes, not dollars
#    across tickers. The ledger above is the point: capital weights and
#    exposures are different objects.
# 3. CAPITAL EFFICIENCY. A modestly levered balanced mix beats a
#    concentrated equity portfolio at equal risk (Asness, "Why Not 100%
#    Equities", JPM 1996; Frazzini & Pedersen, "Betting Against Beta",
#    JFE 2014: leverage aversion leaves the diversified mix underpriced).
#    Return stacking (Hoffstein, Butler & Gordillo; WisdomTree Efficient
#    Core) is the packaged form: NTSG 90/60, GDE 90/90, and a
#    zero-coupon's duration-per-euro on the defensive sleeve.
#
# ## Regime map: what hurts, and which head answers
#
#   Growth boom            ~46% equity across three engines (NTSG world,
#                          ZPRV small value, GDE US large): present, but
#                          deliberately less than a 60/40's equity book.
#   Disinflation/recession ~11% intermediate duration (NTSG overlay) plus
#                          trend rotating long duration.
#   Deflation crash (2008) ZROZ long-end convexity + trend short risk
#                          assets + unhedged USD flight-to-quality.
#   Inflation shock        Trend long commodities/short bonds (Neville,
#                          Draaisma, Funnell, Harvey & van Hemert, "The
#                          Best Strategies for Inflationary Times", JPM
#                          2021) + 18% gold via GDE.
#   Choppy stagflation     Gold (trend whipsaws without a trend; Neville
#                          et al. 2021).
#   Prolonged equity bear  Trend crisis alpha (Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen, "A
#                          Century of Evidence on Trend-Following
#                          Investing", JPM 2017).
#
#   Measured coverage (share of weight): growth 46%, inflation 70%,
#   crisis 68%, deflation 21%, against the buyable hydra's 44/57/53/29.
#   The trend budget buys inflation and crisis presence; DEFLATION is this
#   build's thin quadrant, and knowingly so: the previous version rented
#   its bond book from RSBT's stacked core, which the measurement below
#   says was not worth its price. Everything defensive now rides on 10
#   points of zero-coupon plus NTSG's overlay. The ZROZ-15 variant in the
#   range check is the repair if that trade-off feels wrong.
#
# ## The five engines: rationale, sane ranges, UCITS status
#
# ENGINE 1 -- NTSG, equity + duration (18%; sane range 10-30%).
#   90% developed-world equities + 60% sovereign futures in one line.
#   Already UCITS (IE00077IIPQ8); the euro-native NTSZ (IE000OV4XWA3) is
#   the FX-aware variant. This is the one engine that survives unchanged
#   in the buyable sibling. Its weight is the file's main a-priori
#   decision: the sweep pays MORE Sharpe and MORE CAGR for cutting it to
#   zero, and what it buys instead is the FLOOR (worst rolling 5y rises
#   from 0.68% at 0% NTSG to 1.21% at 35%) plus the world-equity presence
#   that pays a 30-year plan. 18% is where those two arguments meet.
#
# ENGINE 2 -- ZPRV, small-cap value (12%; sane range 5-15%).
#   Size and value premia (Fama & French 1992/1993) in the value-weighted
#   form that avoids the small-growth lottery corner (Asness, Frazzini,
#   Israel, Moskowitz & Pedersen, "Size Matters, If You Control Your
#   Junk", JFE 2018). Deepest clean backcast of the book (DFSVX spliced
#   onto Ken French small-value, 1963). Already UCITS (IE00BSPLC413);
#   global variant AVWS (IE0003R87OG3). It is the most expensive sleeve in
#   risk: every 5 points added costs ~1.5 points of drawdown and ~0.35
#   points of worst-5y, which is why it stays a tilt and not a core.
#
# ENGINES 3a/3b -- trend, TWO models (20% + 20%; range 25-45% total, no
#   single leg above ~22%).
#   Time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, JFE 2012) is the one
#   head that can profit in both tails, and the sleeve with the worst
#   single-model risk, so it is split across two genuinely different
#   methods. Engine 3a is DBi's SG CTA replication in its EUR-HEDGED share
#   class (MFEH, LU3359622902, UCITS, 0.75%/yr): it infers the industry's
#   aggregate position by regression. Engine 3b is direct TSMOM run by the
#   manager itself (AQR Managed Futures UCITS, RAEF EUR LU1662501532),
#   which computes its own signals across the futures universe. Both legs
#   are UCITS and both are EUR-hedged, which is most of why they beat the
#   dollar-denominated route for a euro investor (see the ledger below).
#   Alternatives: the unhedged EUR class DBMFE (LU2951555403), Winton
#   (IE00BG382Q20), or AQR's flat-fee I class (IAE1FT LU2622190622) for
#   large tickets.
#
# ENGINE 4 -- GDE, gold stacked on equity (20%; sane range 10-25%).
#   Gold alone has near-zero expected real return (Erb & Harvey, "The
#   Golden Dilemma", FAJ 2013) -- the classic argument for keeping it
#   small. GDE dodges the drag by stacking 90% gold futures on 90% S&P
#   500, so the sleeve keeps earning the equity premium while carrying
#   the debasement/stagflation hedge. It is the CAGR engine of this build
#   (the per-sleeve sweep buys +0.33 pts of CAGR per 5 points of GDE, all
#   the way to 45%, and it lifts the worst-5y floor too), and its price is
#   ~0.5 pts of volatility per 5 points and an ever more US-large equity
#   book. NOT UCITS, and no UCITS twin exists: the fallback is a physical
#   ETC (IGLN IE00B4ND3602) at ~0.9x, accepting the drag GDE was dodging.
#
# ENGINE 5 -- ZROZ, deep deflation duration (10%; sane range 5-15%).
#   ~27y duration per euro vs ~17 for a 20+y fund: capital efficiency
#   applied to crash insurance. NTSG carries intermediate duration; this
#   is the long end, which is what actually rallies in a 2008. With the
#   stacked bond leg gone it is also the build's whole nominal-bond book,
#   which is why its weight doubled from the RSBT-era 5%. NOT UCITS;
#   route: IDTL (IE00BSKRJZ44) at ~1.6x, or EUR-hedged DTLE to strip the
#   USD from the defensive sleeve.
#
# ## How far the shape can be pushed (frontier, 1996-03 -> 2026-08, EUR)
#   The question this file exists to answer is "how much CAGR per unit of
#   volatility can these engines deliver". Maximum CAGR reachable under a
#   volatility cap, searched over the six sleeves with sane per-sleeve
#   caps (daily-return volatility, the report's headline number):
#     vol <= 8.0%  ->  CAGR  9.4%     vol <= 9.5%  ->  CAGR 10.8%
#     vol <= 8.5%  ->  CAGR 10.0%     vol <= 10.0% ->  CAGR 11.1%
#     vol <= 9.0%  ->  CAGR 10.4%     vol <= 10.5% ->  CAGR 11.4%
#   Two readings matter. (1) "CAGR above 10% at under 8% volatility" is
#   NOT reachable here: 8% volatility caps the CAGR near 9.4%, and matching
#   the S&P 500's own 10.76%/yr (at its 20.9% volatility) needs at least
#   ~9.5% of volatility. Half the index's volatility for the same return is
#   the honest ceiling of the build; a quarter of it is not on offer.
#   (2) Those corner solutions are overfit by construction (they run trend
#   to 32%+ on one leg, NTSG to 4%, GDE to 28%). The shipped weights sit
#   ~0.2-0.3 pts of CAGR below the unconstrained frontier at their own
#   volatility, and that gap is the premium paid for weights that are
#   defensible sleeve by sleeve rather than fitted to one panel.
#
# ## Alternatives on the plain sleeves, checked and rejected
#   MFEH, RAEF, ZPRV and ZROZ are the sleeves NOT return-stacked. The
#   test: swap each for a stacked instrument carrying the same core plus a
#   second exposure, same weights, common window, EUR:
#   - Trend -> RSBT (100% US core bonds + 100% trend). This was engine 3a
#     until the measurement went the other way: on the shared 2000-2026
#     window, moving 6, 10 or 14 points into RSBT (from the trend leg, from
#     the zero-coupon, or from both) costs Sharpe monotonically (0.94 -> 0.91 -> 0.90 -> 0.88), adds
#     volatility and deepens the drawdown every time. RSBT alone over
#     1996-2026 reads CAGR 4.99%, vol 15.4%, Sharpe 0.39 in EUR, against
#     6.70%, 11.9% and 0.59 for MFEH: the free bond leg does not pay for
#     the 1.02%/yr fee stack, the unhedged dollar, or the 1.5x notional.
#     The stacked bonds are cheaper bought as ZROZ plus NTSG's overlay.
#   - Trend -> RSST (US stocks + trend) or Winton Trend Enhanced Global
#     Equity (MSCI World + trend). Re-tested on the new mix: a free search
#     that may hold up to 30% RSST gives it 0 to 1.5%. The book already
#     carries ~46 points of equity; the trend sleeve earns its keep
#     precisely by NOT adding more.
#   - Small-cap value (ZPRV): no stacked SCV instrument exists. The
#     nearest same-core alternative is global SCV. Re-measured in this mix
#     over 1996-2026, AVWS (IE0003R87OG3) instead of ZPRV reads CAGR
#     10.69% / vol 9.76% / Sharpe 1.06 against 10.77% / 9.89% / 1.05: a
#     wash, and a 6/6 split lands exactly in between. Alone, AVWS is the
#     better-behaved sleeve (Sharpe 0.62 vs 0.59, drawdown -62% vs -64%,
#     worst 5y -8.5% vs -11.3%) for 0.4 pts less CAGR. The tie-breakers
#     are outside the backtest and point both ways: AVWS carries a 31%
#     non-US tail, which is the one real answer to blind spot (f), while
#     ZPRV costs 0.09%/yr less and keeps the deeper clean backcast (1963 vs
#     1994, below which global SCV proxies non-US with US data). ZPRV
#     stays; AVWS is a legitimate swap, not an upgrade.
#   - Gold (inside GDE) -> IGLN, physical gold at 1x. A free search gives
#     IGLN only 2.5 to 5 points; forced doses replace equity premium with
#     nothing, so both CAGR and volatility fall together and Sharpe barely
#     moves (10 points of GDE moved to IGLN: CAGR 10.77% -> 10.09%, vol
#     9.89% -> 9.07%, Sharpe 1.05 -> 1.07; the full swap: 9.38% / 8.56% /
#     1.06). That is a slide DOWN the same frontier line, not a better
#     frontier, and it is exactly the Erb-Harvey drag GDE exists to dodge.
#     IGLN belongs in the buyable sibling, where GDE is not an option.
#   - Euro inflation-linked ballast -> CHSN (LU1645380442, euro linkers
#     1-10, backcast from 2005-11). On its own shorter window, 5, 10 or 15
#     points of CHSN cost 0.4, 0.8 and 1.2 pts of CAGR for the same Sharpe
#     (0.93 against 0.93). A free search does push it to ~29 points and
#     Sharpe 1.02, but at 6.8% CAGR, and READ THAT WITH CARE: this
#     toolkit's Sharpe uses a ZERO risk-free rate, so any cash-like sleeve
#     buys ratio for free. CHSN is ballast, and its real job (euro
#     inflation and liquidity near a departure date) is a decumulation
#     buffer question, not a frontier question. Out of this file, in the
#     fire-* examples.
#   - Long Treasury (ZROZ): a 25+y zero-coupon is ALREADY the most
#     capital-efficient long-duration expression. Nothing to swap to.
#   Net: the plain sleeves are plain on purpose. Stacking is used where it
#   helps (growth, duration and gold cores), not on the crisis hedges.
#
# ## Wider-universe search on the same window
#   Adding RSSB (stacked global stocks+bonds), Simplify CTA (a third trend
#   model), physical gold, intermediate Treasuries and broad commodities to
#   the search set moves the attainable frontier by almost nothing: best
#   Sharpe 1.14 against 1.13 for the six sleeves alone, and no gain at all
#   in CAGR under a 9.5% volatility cap (10.66% found with them, 10.79%
#   without). Each newcomer draws 0 to 5 points and duplicates an engine
#   already there. The engine list, not the ticker list, sets this frontier.
#   Two older findings still hold: the USD 25+y zero-coupon beats the euro
#   15-30y govt sleeve on CAGR, Sharpe, worst-5y and drawdown over
#   2008-2026 (~16y duration vs 27, and no USD flight-to-quality kick), and
#   dropping ZPRV's value tilt for broad small caps loses on both Sharpe
#   and worst-5y.
#
# ## Currency: why both trend legs are hedged
#   MFEH and DBMFE are the same sub-fund in two share classes, so their
#   difference IS the EUR hedge: over the 1996-2026 backcast in EUR,
#   6.70%/yr at 11.9% volatility hedged against 6.59% at 15.1% unhedged
#   (max drawdown -24.5% vs -29.2%). An unhedged managed-futures sleeve is
#   trend PLUS a long-dollar position, and the dollar leg adds three
#   points of volatility for no measured return. Hedging is not free
#   looking forward: the cost is the USD-EUR carry gap, ~1.5%/yr at
#   2026-08 rates and near zero over 2000-2014. The build accepts that
#   because the sleeve's whole job is to pay in euros whatever the dollar
#   does in the crisis that triggers it.
#
# ## Blind spots
#
#   (a) DEFLATION IS THE THIN QUADRANT (21% of weight vs 29% for the
#       buyable hydra). A disinflationary bust that trend does not catch in
#       time leans on 10 points of ZROZ and 11 of NTSG overlay.
#   (b) Embedded financing cost: the ~25 stacked points pay T-bill inside
#       the funds. When cash is at 5% that is ~1.3%/yr of drag before the
#       overlays earn anything, exactly when bonds and equities may both
#       struggle (2022-23). Invisible on a statement, real in the return.
#   (c) Fee stack: 0.33%/yr weighted TER vs 0.29% for the buyable hydra.
#       The trend legs are the expensive lines (0.75% and 0.23%); AQR's
#       flat-fee I class cuts engine 3b further for tickets that clear its
#       minimum.
#   (d) Product risk: GDE is a small, young ETF, and MFEH is younger still
#       (a few weeks of live history, ~EUR 11m of assets, Xetra only, not
#       yet registered for sale in France at the time of writing). A
#       closure or a mandate change forces an unplanned redesign and a
#       taxable event; check MFEH's size and registration before holding
#       it, and keep DBMFE (unhedged) or the USD class in view.
#   (e) A fast joint stock/bond selloff that whipsaws trend before it
#       flips short: with 40 points of capital in trend, this build leans
#       harder on trend reacting in time than any of its siblings.
#   (f) Currency: the two trend legs are EUR-hedged, everything else is
#       USD-quoted and unhedged for a EUR investor; euro-area inflation
#       only hedged indirectly; no euro liquid sleeve for decumulation.
#       Same patches as the buyable hydra (NTSZ, DTLE, euro linkers near
#       departure).
#   (g) Reconstruction risk: most of the deep history is backcast (SIM),
#       both trend legs share one reference in their pre-fund era, and the
#       trend backcasts understate live manager dispersion. A 40-point
#       trend budget is exactly where that flattery bites hardest. Treat
#       the backtest as a regime-behavior study, not a promise.
#
# ## Honesty note on the WEIGHTS
#   The sleeves and their roles are theory-first; the exact percentages
#   are backtest-informed (grid and random search over 1996-2026, checked
#   for stability on the 2000, 2008, 2015 and 2020 sub-windows, then
#   hand-tempered). Expect the measured edge to shrink out of sample; the
#   engine logic and the ranges are the part built to survive. RANGE CHECK
#   (per-sleeve sweep on THIS build, rest renormalized, 1996-2026):
#   confirmed empirically -- ZPRV 5-15 (past 15, drawdown and worst-5y
#   degrade fast: -35.6% and -1.77% by 45%), the GDE range 10-25 (CAGR and
#   worst-5y keep rising past it, Sharpe peaks near 10% and volatility
#   makes the call), ZROZ 5-15 (Sharpe peaks near 5-10%, the worst-5y
#   floor keeps improving to 30% while CAGR and volatility both get worse),
#   the NTSG CEILING near 30% (Sharpe 1.00 and falling, drawdown -21%).
#   Still A PRIORI: the NTSG FLOOR (the sweep prefers 0% and pays for it;
#   what NTSG buys is the worst-5y floor and forward-looking world-equity
#   presence, not backtest Sharpe) and the trend CEILING (the sweep is
#   happiest at 40-45% on either leg, which is in-sample flattery of a
#   shared reconstruction; 40% total is already at the edge of what the
#   live record supports).
#   Two documented variants of the same shape:
#     deflation-repaired  17 NTSG / 11 ZPRV / 19 MFEH / 19 RAEF / 19 GDE /
#                         15 ZROZ: CAGR 10.56%, vol 9.75%, drawdown -18.5%,
#                         worst 5y +1.27%. Trades 0.2 pts of CAGR for a
#                         thicker bond book and a higher floor.
#     Sharpe-first        16 NTSG / 12 ZPRV / 22 MFEH / 22 RAEF / 20 GDE /
#                         8 ZROZ: CAGR 10.77%, vol 9.71%, Sharpe 1.07. Same
#                         return, slightly less volatility, and 44 points
#                         of trend, i.e. more of the flattered sleeve.
#
