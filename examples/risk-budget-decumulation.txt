# Risk-budget decumulation -- every weight carries the reason it exists.
#
# STATUS: implementable today from a French retail setup (a taxable account
#   plus the equity-only tax shelters). Same family as
#   dragon-decumulation-household, one generation further: the equity block
#   moves into the tax shelters as far as the RISK budget allows, the bond
#   overlay lost on the way is rebuilt with two issuers instead of one, and
#   every line is labelled by the kind of claim that justifies it.
#
# Thesis in one line: start from the liability (a stream of real euros over
#   ~40 years), immunize the years where a bad sequence is unrecoverable,
#   and spend the surplus on mechanisms that pay a documented premium, not
#   on assets that merely backtested well.
#
# LABELS. Each weight below is tagged by what produced it:
#   DERIVED    falls out of a calculation (the linkers dose).
#   MECHANISM  a documented premium; the dose is a judgement call.
#   HEDGE      no premium at all; the dose is what the covered regime
#              demands (gold, long duration).
#   CONSTRAINT set by a wrapper's rules or by an assumed behavioral limit.
#
# ## Lineage
#
#   Browne's Permanent Portfolio (1981) -> Cole's Dragon (Artemis, 2020)
#   -> claude-dragonlite (three buyable UCITS around a 90/60 core) ->
#   hydra-five-engines (one line per return engine) ->
#   dragon-decumulation-household (the same design across French wrappers)
#   -> here. The step this file adds is the liability: the earlier files
#   size sleeves against a portfolio, this one sizes them against a
#   spending stream, and the two answers differ.
#
# ## 1. The numeraire: the risk-free asset is not cash
#
#   Merton, then Campbell & Viceira (Strategic Asset Allocation, 2002):
#   the risk-free asset is the one whose cash flow replicates the
#   LIABILITY. The liability here is real euros for decades. Cash is not
#   risk-free against it (it loses to inflation); a ladder of euro
#   inflation-linked bonds is.
#
#   Consequence 1, how to read the linkers line. Its low expected return
#   IS its function. It is the zero point from which every other line is a
#   deliberate departure that a premium has to pay for. Its job is not to
#   earn, but to guarantee that the floor years it covers arrive intact
#   whatever inflation does. Judge it like insurance, on service rendered,
#   never by comparing its return to equities.
#
#   Consequence 2, why 1-10y and not all maturities. Duration ~4 against
#   ~7-8 for an all-maturity fund: 2022 showed long linkers losing 25-35%
#   DURING an inflation shock (real-rate duration), exactly when they were
#   being counted on. Short maturities buy the indexation without the
#   real-rate bet. An all-maturity linker fund is a real-rate bet, not an
#   inflation hedge.
#
# ## 2. Sizing the linkers by horizon (DERIVED)
#
#   Sequence risk only destroys on the near years, where no recovery is
#   possible; distant years have the horizon working for them. So immunize
#   the beginning and take the risk on the rest. At a floor drawn near
#   ~2.5-3%/yr of capital and discounted at the real yield linkers
#   actually pay (~1%):
#     years 1-3    covered by the cash buffer, held OUTSIDE this file
#     years 4-10   ~12-13% of capital          <- the dose retained
#     years 4-15   about double that
#     years 4-40   ~57%: Bodie's answer, ruin impossible, growth
#                  abandoned. Refused.
#   A household that already owns indexed annuities (state pension,
#   indexed rent, a residual salary) subtracts their present value from
#   the floor first: owning them is what lets a floor worth ~45% of
#   capital be covered by ~13% of linkers. Note that such an annuity
#   starting years out does NOT shrink this dose, because it starts after
#   the immunized window; the derivation is invariant to it, which is a
#   good reason not to reopen this line at every pension scare.
#
# ## 3. The premia that actually exist
#
#   Risk is only paid for against a mechanism, never against a track record.
#   EQUITY     covariance with consumption. The widest premium, the
#              soundest mechanism. Anchor its expected return on CAPE
#              (~3% real from today's level), not on the historical mean.
#   TREND      the most robust documented anomaly: a century, every asset
#              class (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen 2012; Hurst, Ooi &
#              Pedersen 2017). Mechanism: under-reaction plus risk
#              transfer. Correlation ~0 to equities, negative in crises.
#              CAVEAT: that century is gross of fees, on paper backtests.
#              Investable UCITS trend (0.8-1.3% plus replication error),
#              net, has a markedly thinner realized Sharpe and an enormous
#              manager dispersion. The premium exists; its NET SIZE is the
#              least certain input in the whole design. Measured on this
#              file's own backcast window (2005-2025, real EUR): the
#              replication leg earned ~3.8%/yr real and the direct-manager
#              leg ~1.2%, an excess over cash of ~3.6%/yr for the pair.
#              The dispersion is not hypothetical; it is the thing the
#              two-leg structure diversifies.
#   TERM       thin today. The line stands on its hedging demand (negative
#              beta to growth shocks), not on its yield.
#   SIZE/VALUE Fama-French, contested since 2000: weaker mechanism, hence
#              a minority share INSIDE the equity sleeve.
#   GOLD       zero real expected return (Erb & Harvey 2013). Not a
#              premium, an insurance, and LOGICALLY THE SAME STATUS as
#              long duration: held for its covariance with bad states of
#              the world. Size it by the regime it covers (section 7),
#              not by a "minimum polite allocation".
#
# ## 4. The risk budget, and the behavioral cap on trend
#
#   Capital shares are not risk shares, and this file is named after the
#   difference. Capital 42/20/15/13/10 (equity / trend / gold / linkers /
#   duration) sits, EX ANTE (standalone volatilities and assumed
#   correlations), at roughly 61/23/9/3/4 of variance. That is the budget
#   the design was written against.
#   MEASURED EX POST, the report's risk-budget block (Euler decomposition
#   of variance on the simulation's monthly contributions, over this
#   file's window) says something different and worth reading twice:
#     equity 65% of variance for 42 of capital and 57% of realized return
#     gold 15% for 15 and 22%
#     trend 11% for 20 and 13%
#     govt bonds 6% for 10 and 5%
#     linkers 3% for 13 and 3%
#   Two lessons. First, a book balanced in CAPITAL across regimes is still
#   an equity book in RISK: two thirds of the variance sits in one sleeve,
#   which is exactly what the defensive lines exist to blunt, and the
#   number to watch when the equity band drifts. Second, the ex-ante 23%
#   for trend was an overstatement: an Euler share prices MARGINAL
#   contribution, so a sleeve that moves independently of the book
#   consumes far less variance than its standalone volatility suggests.
#   Twenty points of trend cost 11% of the risk budget here; the cap below
#   is therefore a behavioral limit, not a risk-budget constraint, and
#   should be argued as one. Gold, meanwhile, is not free: it takes as
#   much variance as it takes capital, and in this window it earned it.
#   Re-run the block after any weight change (it is the "Risk budget" row
#   of the HTML report) rather than trusting the ex-ante figures above.
#
#   Within that budget, unconstrained optimization would push trend
#   toward 30-45 and gold lower. Refused, for two independent reasons:
#   (a) uncertainty on trend's NET premium (section 3): moving 14 points
#       of risk budget onto the least certain input is optimization under
#       another name;
#   (b) asymmetry of the errors under uncertainty about one's own
#       behavior. Nobody knows in advance how they will sit through a
#       2011-2019 flat stretch in a trend sleeve. A trend leg too small
#       costs a few basis points of missed diversification, invisible in
#       the floor metrics; a trend leg too big, abandoned at the bottom,
#       costs a permanent capitulation on a third of the book at the worst
#       possible moment. Stand where being wrong is cheap: 20.
#   REVISION PROTOCOL: revisable UPWARD on lived evidence only (keep a
#   decision journal through each flat or losing stretch), 20 -> 25 at an
#   annual review only, NEVER during a drawdown of the sleeve, and no cut
#   during a drawdown either. Mitigations: judge trend only at the whole
#   book level, never the line alone; look quarterly, not daily. Base
#   rate: Morningstar's "Mind the Gap" measures ~1-2%/yr lost to timing by
#   investors who all thought they were steady.
#   QUANTIFIED (2026-08): a decumulation Monte-Carlo sweep of the trend
#   weight over {7.5, 12, 17, 22} improves the floor metrics MONOTONICALLY
#   up to 22 in five of six return models (years at the floor in a lost
#   decade: 6.8 -> 2.8 going from 7.5 to 17); the only model that pays is
#   the flat-CTA decade, and only its median (-0.24 pt/yr), never its
#   tail. The regret asymmetry argued above is measured, not just pleaded,
#   and the upward-only revision protocol has numbers behind it.
#
# ## 5. The bond tent that does not say its name
#
#   42 points of equity at the start is not "too defensive": it is a
#   rising equity glidepath (Kitces & Pfau 2014) taken deliberately inside
#   the retirement red zone (~5 years before, ~10 after the exit). The
#   defensive start is a TRAJECTORY, not a state: past year ~8, stop
#   refilling the linkers and let equity drift up by not rebalancing it
#   down. Write that in the household's rule page: "no DOWNWARD
#   rebalancing of equity after year 8". Consequence: any comparison of
#   expected return against a static book UNDERSTATES this one, because
#   the return given up is concentrated on the years where it buys the
#   most survival.
#   GUARDRAIL PLUMBING that goes with the tent: if the plan includes an
#   indexed income arriving mid-retirement (a state pension), REBASE the
#   guardrail reference rate at each income step. Without the rebase, the
#   pension's arrival mechanically crushes the measured withdrawal rate
#   and a prosperity rule would raise spending for the wrong reason.
#   Name the mechanical loss honestly: against a build centered on a
#   stacked 90/60 core, notional exposure falls from ~130% to ~116%,
#   because cheap embedded leverage was handed back when the equity moved
#   into the shelters. If the floor metrics ever suffer for it, recover
#   leverage at the margin with +1-2 points of long duration (duration is
#   cheap leverage), never with more equity.
#
# ## 6. The deflation leg: two issuers, the dollar left unhedged
#
#   The bond overlay lost with the stacked core is rebuilt by TWO long
#   duration lines, half and half, so the whole leg does not sit on one
#   sovereign complex. This is the only place in the book where the dollar
#   is an asset rather than a risk:
#   1. Two engines add up. In a growth crisis the dollar rises (haven) AND
#      Treasuries rise (flight to quality); a euro holder collects both.
#      2008 measured: about +26% for the unhedged US line against about
#      +7% for euro 25+. The euro sovereign drags its spread risk in
#      exactly when it is needed (2011 showed it again).
#   2. Hedging pays nothing: a EUR-hedged US bond earns the euro rate. The
#      carry buys the removal of currency variance, nothing else.
#   3. The currency risk taken in exchange is small and BOUNDED: 5 points
#      of dollar against an all-euro floor (linkers, euro long duration,
#      the cash buffer, the hedged trend leg).
#   CAVEAT, written for the future reader: the "crisis means USD and
#   Treasuries rally together" playbook assumes a crisis NOT centered on
#   the United States. The 2025 tariff episodes showed the dollar and long
#   Treasuries falling in concert when the shock is US fiscal and
#   institutional: the line can then lose on both legs at once. That is
#   precisely why it is capped at 5 and never averaged up. And 2025 was
#   not the first failure, only the first watched live: in the 1990
#   oil-shock recession (US-centered: S&L, recession, dollar sliding
#   against the mark) the unhedged US line printed -6.7% in EUR while the
#   euro long line printed +5.9%. Both times, the half that paid was the
#   liability-currency half. The two-issuer split is not diversification
#   cosmetics; it is what has actually carried the leg through both of
#   its measured failures.
#   Accepted corollary: outside crises the line moves with EURUSD,
#   visible volatility with no expected return attached. That is the
#   premium on an insurance that pays twice when it pays.
#
# ## 7. Gold at 15, not 10
#
#   Cutting gold to a token 10 ("no premium, minimum dose") while keeping
#   long duration for its hedging demand applies the same logic
#   selectively. Gold stays at 15 because the regime it covers (long
#   stagflation, monetary debasement) is the worst-covered regime in the
#   whole book (Neville, Draaisma, Funnell, Harvey & van Hemert, JPM
#   2021): long duration LOSES there (2022), and trend is slow to turn and
#   is exactly the sleeve capped for behavioral reasons above. Gold and
#   trend are the only two legs of that regime; weakening both at once is
#   incoherent. The dose is what the regime demands GIVEN that the other
#   leg is capped.
#   HEAD-TO-HEAD CHECK (2026-08): a leaner variant at 9 points of gold was
#   dominated by this file across six decumulation return models,
#   INCLUDING one where every sleeve is capped at prudent forward priors
#   (gold at 1%/yr real). Gold is paid by its covariance with bad states,
#   not by its 2005-2025 tailwind (~9%/yr real, not to be extrapolated).
#   An isolated weight sweep cannot see this (8 vs 11 reads as noise);
#   only the full head-to-head, in the tails, does.
#   WHAT GOLD DOES NOT COVER (1990, measured on the deep-history file):
#   gold is a DOLLAR asset, and in an inflation shock where the dollar
#   falls it can lose double digits for a euro holder. Through the 1990
#   oil-spike recession, the worst drawdown of the whole forty-year test,
#   gold printed -13.0% in EUR (only -3.1% in USD; the rest is the
#   dollar) while hedged trend printed +16.0% and was the one leg that
#   paid. Read the division of labor precisely: TREND catches the shock
#   as it happens (long energy, short bonds: +16% in 1990, +15% in 2022
#   for the net index); GOLD covers the monetary aftermath and the
#   debasement decade, on its own clock. Judging gold on the shock year
#   is the same mistake as judging the linkers on 2022.
#
#   42 equity (of which 14 small value)
#   + ~26.5 nominal duration in 7y-equivalent terms (the euro 25+ line at
#     ~20y duration counts ~2.9x its 5 points, the US 20+ at ~17y ~2.4x)
#   + 20 trend + 15 gold + 13 linkers  = ~116% of exposure, no margin.
#   Against a stacked-core build, equivalent duration is HIGHER (~26.5 vs
#   ~16 points) and concentrated on the very long end: more convex per
#   euro, and better matched to a euro liability for the euro half.
#   Accept the consequence: a ~20y duration line will print -30% in
#   another 2022. That is the line doing its job, and the anti-panic rule
#   must name it explicitly ("do not sell duration after a rate shock").
#   Currency: ~5 points of dollar through the unhedged US leg, ~10 more
#   riding the replication trend line (its EUR share class is UNHEDGED:
#   the NAV carries EURUSD on the whole position, correlation 0.70 with
#   the dollar twin), plus the USD inside the global small-value fund;
#   the floor stays in euros. The 1990 measurement prices that trend
#   side-effect: in a falling-dollar crisis the unhedged half returns
#   most of its crisis alpha to the currency, exactly when trend is the
#   only leg that pays. A hedged sister class exists since 2026-05
#   (MFEH, LU3359622902, same portfolio, same 0.75%/yr; measured on the
#   backcast it trades ~0.75 pt of CAGR for 3 points less volatility and
#   ~5 points less drawdown); switch when its assets and registrations
#   allow.
#
# ## 9. Regime map: what hurts a spending household, what answers
#
#   Growth boom            42 points of equity: presence. The small value
#                          share diversifies the mega-cap concentration a
#                          world index carries.
#   Deflation crash        The two long duration lines are bought for this
#                          and nothing else, one in the liability currency,
#                          one collecting the haven dollar on top. Trend
#                          rotates long bonds as the move extends.
#   Disinflation/recession Duration again, plus trend; the linkers keep
#                          their value and pay the floor meanwhile.
#   Trending inflation     Trend short bonds and long commodities (Neville
#                          et al. 2021) plus the linkers accruing the index
#                          by contract. The 2022 configuration.
#   Choppy inflation       Gold and the linkers' accrual. The worst covered
#   (stagflation)          regime and the retiree killer: trend is slow to
#                          turn, duration loses outright. Section 7. The
#                          1990 measurement (deep-history file) adds a
#                          twist: when the shock comes with a FALLING
#                          dollar, gold fails too (in EUR) and trend
#                          alone answers; see section 7.
#   Long equity bear       Trend crisis alpha (Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen 2017);
#                          linkers and duration are what gets spent while
#                          equity is underwater, which is what stops the
#                          sequence from becoming permanent.
#   US-centered fiscal     The uncovered corner, deliberately: the unhedged
#   shock                  US leg can lose on both its legs at once (2025),
#                          hence the cap of 5. Gold is the only answer.
#
# ## 10. Sane ranges, and what leaving them costs
#
#   Ranges are derived from the roles above, then checked against the
#   backtest, never read off it.
#   Equity 42 (35-50). Below 35 the growth engine no longer carries a
#     40-year horizon; above 50 the equity variance share (already ~65%)
#     leaves the other lines nothing to blunt, which defeats the tent.
#   Trend 20 (15-25). Below 15 the sleeve stops moving the book after
#     fees; 25 is the behavioral ceiling of section 4, not a risk one.
#   Gold 15 (10-20). Below 10 the stagflation regime loses one of its only
#     two legs; above 20 a zero-real-return asset is funded out of the
#     growth engine.
#   Linkers 13 (10-20). 10 covers roughly the floor to year 8, 20 to year
#     15; beyond that the book drifts toward Bodie's abandoned-growth
#     corner.
#   Long duration 10, split 5/5 (8-14 total, the unhedged US leg NEVER
#     above 5). The total rebuilds the overlay lost with the stacked core;
#     the split is issuer diversification, the cap is section 6.
#   Small value 14 inside the 42 of equity (10-18). A contested mechanism
#     gets a minority share of the sleeve, about a third here.
#
# ## 11. Blind spots, stated rather than discovered later
#
#   NO LONG VOLATILITY, the dragon's fifth leg. A crash that completes in
#     days (February 2018, March 2020) is covered by nothing here: trend
#     needs weeks to reposition, gold is unreliable in the first sessions,
#     duration only helps if the shock is deflationary. Accepted because
#     listed long-vol vehicles bleed structurally and a household cannot
#     run an option book with discipline. UPGRADED FROM ASSUMED TO
#     MEASURED (2026-08): a 3-point long-vol sleeve was simulated in the
#     decumulation Monte-Carlo; near-neutral under generous assumptions
#     (-4%/yr bleed, +90% payoff on a <=-30% equity year), NEGATIVE under
#     a realistic one (-10%/yr bleed, +60%). And the retail UCITS shelf
#     was surveyed: the listed VIX-roll product lost ~99% since 2012, the
#     genuine tail funds are institutional-access. Trend buys roughly
#     three times the protection at positive carry.
#   NO BROAD COMMODITIES beyond gold: an energy shock is reached only
#     through the trend sleeve, which has to catch it first. ALSO MEASURED
#     (2026-08): a 2-point broad-commodity sleeve was tested head-to-head
#     and dragged every model (-2.2%/yr real over 2005-2025, and a prudent
#     prior only caps upside tailwinds, it never lifts a drag).
#   THE EQUITY SLEEVE IS A US MEGA-CAP BET in disguise (world cap weight),
#     and small value is only a partial answer to it.
#   CONCENTRATION IN NAMES, not just in assets: two managers carry the
#     whole trend sleeve, one of them priced at NAV rather than traded,
#     and the sheltered world fund is swap based. Counterparty and manager
#     risk are the price of the wrapper and of the diversification.
#   TAXES ARE OUTSIDE THE BACKTEST entirely, while they are half the
#     argument for where each line is held.
#   THE FLOOR IS IN EUROS and much of the equity is not: a long EUR
#     appreciation is a real drag that no line here offsets.
#   CONSIDERED AND REJECTED: all-maturity linkers (a real-rate bet, not an
#     inflation hedge), EUR-hedged long Treasuries (distributing, and the
#     hedge pays the euro yield anyway), keeping a stacked 90/60 core in
#     the taxable account (restores leverage but wastes the shelters), a
#     single trend manager (manager risk on the least certain premium),
#     cash instead of linkers (see section 1).
#   CONSIDERED, MEASURED AND REJECTED (2026-08, on the long-history
#     variants): a broad-commodity sleeve, 4 points carved out of gold as
#     a standing oil-shock hedge. Over 1996-2026 it costs 0.21 points of
#     CAGR a year, deepens the real drawdown from -19.4% to -21.8% (it
#     loses a third of its value across 2008) and drops the worst rolling
#     5y from 0.80% to 0.28%, for about +0.6 points in 2022: a premium
#     paid every year for a hedge that pays once a decade, and whose
#     exposure the trend sleeve already RENTS when it trends (+16% in
#     1990, +15% in 2022 for the net index). An oil shock is a spike that
#     reverses (WTI +112% June-October 1990, then -29% in 1991), not a
#     state to hold. Also measured and refused: floating-rate cash in
#     place of part of the linker line (nil effect: 13 points at duration
#     4.5 weigh 0.6 duration-points on the whole book).
#
# ## 12. Backtesting this file
#
#   RE-DERIVED AND STRESSED (2026-08). As an adversarial check, the same
#     liability was handed to three blind designers from opposed schools
#     (liability-driven, all-weather risk-balanced, capital-efficient
#     return-stacking), plus an instrument-universe survey, WITHOUT access
#     to this file. Their synthesis reconverged on this architecture: the
#     same two trend vehicles, the same euro linkers fund, the same
#     "equity budget first, tax wrapper second" doctrine, ~116% notional.
#     The head-to-head decumulation Monte-Carlo that followed (monthly
#     real-return panel 2005-2025, 5000 paths, six return models including
#     capped prudent priors, floor-years / first-cut / terminal-wealth
#     metrics) kept THIS file dominant over the blind challenger on every
#     model, prudent priors included (lost decade: 2.3 mean years at the
#     floor vs 5.0). The drivers: the small-value tilt, gold at 15, and
#     two drags on the challenger's side (broad commodities, an oversized
#     near-zero-real linkers block). Ruin stayed <= 0.1% for both, which
#     is the saturation argument of section 13 in action.
#
#   WHAT THE WINDOW CAN AND CANNOT SAY. The common period starts 2005-11,
#     bounded by the linkers line. It contains 2008, 2011, 2020 and 2022,
#     and therefore says something about deflation crashes and about a
#     trending-inflation year. It contains no 2000-02 and no 1970s, so the
#     regime this book fears most, a long choppy stagflation, is ABSENT
#     from the sample: the gold and trend doses rest on outside evidence
#     (section 7), not on these twenty years. Measured over the window,
#     pre-tax and before any withdrawal: CAGR ~7.7%, volatility ~8.3%,
#     worst drawdown ~-20%, worst rolling 5y ~+0.5%/yr, weighted ongoing
#     charges 0.58%. Read the drawdown and the worst 5y, not the CAGR.
#     For ruin probabilities rather than a single path, feed the file to
#     `pofo -fire`, which resamples instead of replaying.
#   THE LONG WINDOWS (2026-08, on the sibling files, which substitute the
#     youngest lines for deeper stand-ins and MEASURE the substitution
#     before using it). Three findings worth carrying back here. (1) The
#     dotcom test passes: 2000-03 to 2003-03 at -13.5% against MSCI
#     World's -55.2% in EUR, +2.5% cumulative over 2000-2002 against
#     -44.5%. (2) The forty-year worst drawdown is NOT 2008 and not the
#     dotcom bust: it is 1989-06 to 1991-06 (-20.7%, pessimistically
#     measured), the oil-spike recession, i.e. exactly the regime section
#     7 names as worst covered. The design document predicted where its
#     weakness would be and four decades of data put it there. (3) The
#     passage is paid in advance: 1997-1999 the book compounds +58%
#     against the index's +115%, three straight years of trailing by 12
#     to 24 points BEFORE the three years that repay it. Nobody holds
#     that without having written down, in advance, that it is the plan.
#   ERESMONDEM is the 75/25 blend of two Xtrackers MSCI World ETFs less the
#   FCPE's 0.41%/yr wrapper charge (0.56%/yr all-in per its KID), real NAV
#   from 2024-03, backcast from 1971.
#   DTLA has a clean total-return backcast from 1962. Its EUR-hedged
#     cousin DTLE (IE00BD8PGZ49) is set aside twice over: a distributing class (coupons
#     taxed every year in a taxable account) whose public series is a
#     PRICE return that ruins any backtest, and the hedge converts the US
#     yield into the euro yield anyway. Use DTLETR to look at the hedged
#     segment's total return.
#   CHSN backcasts cleanly to 2005-11, which is what bounds this file's
#     common window. A US TIPS 1-10 stand-in is not a substitute: US CPI
#     base, different series.
#   THE DIRECT TREND SHARE CLASS is chosen on fee PLUMBING, not on past
#     returns, and the reasoning generalises to any multi-class fund
#     (details in pofo docs/aqr-mf.txt). Every EUR class of that fund
#     holds the same portfolio, so comparing two of them on NAV measures
#     their fee difference and never the manager. What each levies INSIDE
#     its NAV: 0.23%/yr for the cheapest class, 0.78% plus 10% of the
#     gains above cash for the performance-fee one, 1.26% flat for the one
#     held here. The flat class wins on two grounds. Its whole cost sits
#     in the quoted price, so the backtest above is what a holder gets.
#     And a performance fee bills the most in the years trend explodes,
#     which are the crash years this sleeve exists to pay the floor
#     through: at +40% it takes 3.7 points against 1.26, surrendering a
#     quarter of the very crisis alpha it was bought for. The cheapest
#     class is cheapest only because its management fee is WAIVED at the
#     manager's sole discretion, revocable without notice, and leaving
#     after a revocation realises a taxable gain. Break-even between the
#     flat and performance-fee classes: an excess of 4.8%/yr over cash.
#   WEIGHTS ARE INTEGERS on purpose. Half a point is worth less than the
#     drift between two rebalancings, and far less than the width of the
#     sane ranges. They are rounded off a design logic, never optimized
#     (DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal, RFS 2009: twenty years of data do not
#     estimate means well enough to beat equal weighting out of sample).
#
# ## 13. Running rules
#
#   - Rebalance on bands, executed in the taxable account; spending IS
#     rebalancing. Inside the shelters the only trade available is
#     equity for equity, so no cross-sleeve move can happen there.
#   - Always use limit orders on the thin LISTED lines (linkers, the
#     replication trend ETF, euro 25+). The direct trend fund is not
#     listed at all: it deals at the day's NAV on a early-afternoon
